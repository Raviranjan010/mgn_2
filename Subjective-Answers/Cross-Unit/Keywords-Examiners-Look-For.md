## UNIT I: WTO, Export Promotion Agencies, Steps & Documentation

### 1. World Trade Organization (WTO)
* **Most-Favored-Nation (MFN) Principle (GATT Art. I):** Non-discrimination among trading partners; immediate and unconditional extension of any trade favor to all members.
* **National Treatment Principle (GATT Art. III):** Equal treatment between imported and domestically produced goods once foreign goods enter the domestic market.
* **Tariff Binding & Concessions (GATT Art. II):** Ceiling commitments on customs tariffs that cannot be unilaterally raised without renegotiation/compensation.
* **Special and Differential Treatment (S&DT):** Preferential provisions, longer implementation periods, and technical assistance for developing and least-developed countries (LDCs).
* **Dispute Settlement Understanding (DSU):** Multilateral mechanism with automatic panel adoption ("Reverse Consensus" / "Negative Consensus").
* **Single Undertaking:** The principle that all WTO multilateral agreements must be accepted by members as an indivisible package ("nothing is agreed until everything is agreed").
* **Agreements:** AoA (Agreement on Agriculture - Amber, Blue, Green boxes), TRIPS (intellectual property, compulsory licensing), TRIMS (investment measures), SPS (sanitary/phytosanitary measures), TBT (technical standards), TFA (Trade Facilitation Agreement).

### 2. Export Promotion Agencies & Institutional Machinery
* **DGFT (Directorate General of Foreign Trade):** Formulates and administers the Foreign Trade Policy (FTP), issues Importer-Exporter Code (IEC), and administers Advance Authorization/EPCG schemes under FT(D&R) Act 1992.
* **EPCs (Export Promotion Councils):** Non-profit industry organizations (e.g., EEPC, CLE, AEPC) that issue Registration-cum-Membership Certificates (RCMC), organize buyer-seller meets, and advise government on sectoral export strategies.
* **Commodity Boards:** Statutory bodies under Ministry of Commerce (Tea Board, Coffee Board, Spices Board, Rubber Board) overseeing production and export development of plantation commodities.
* **APEDA & MPEDA:** Agricultural and Processed Food Products Export Development Authority; Marine Products Export Development Authority (quality standards, phytosanitary compliance, market access).
* **FIEO (Federation of Indian Export Organisations):** Apex coordinating body for all export promotion councils and trade organizations in India.
* **ECGC Ltd. (Export Credit Guarantee Corporation):** Provides export credit insurance policies to exporters (commercial and political risk cover) and financial guarantees to commercial banks.
* **EXIM Bank of India:** Apex financial institution providing Buyer's Credit, Supplier's Credit, Lines of Credit (LoC), and long-term project export financing.
* **EIC / EIA (Export Inspection Council / Agencies):** Statutory agency enforcing pre-shipment quality inspection under Export (Quality Control and Inspection) Act, 1963.
* **IIP (Indian Institute of Packaging):** Certification of UN-marked hazardous cargo packaging, testing, and modern packaging technology.
* **ITPO (India Trade Promotion Organisation):** Organizes domestic and international trade fairs (e.g., India International Trade Fair at Pragati Maidan).

### 3. Steps for Successful Exporting
* **Importer-Exporter Code (IEC):** 10-digit alpha-numeric code (now linked to PAN) issued by DGFT, mandatory for any commercial export/import from India.
* **RCMC (Registration-cum-Membership Certificate):** Certificate issued by an EPC or Commodity Board, mandatory to claim benefits under Foreign Trade Policy.
* **AD Code (Authorized Dealer Code):** 14-digit bank branch identifier registered with Customs via ICEGATE to enable inward remittance linking.
* **Costing & Pricing:** FOB costing, CIF markup, marginal cost pricing, export duty neutralization.
* **Proforma Invoice & Purchase Order:** Commercial offer specifying Incoterm, payment term, specifications, packing, and shipment schedule.
* **Pre-shipment Inspection & Testing:** Statutory certificate of inspection from EIA or private agencies (SGS, Bureau Veritas).
* **Export Realization & Repatriation:** Mandatory repatriation of export proceeds within 9 months via Authorized Dealer Bank under FEMA regulations.

### 4. Export-Import Documentation
* **Aligned Documentation System (ADS):** Standardized documentation framework based on the United Nations Layout Key (UNLK), using standardized A4 size paper and master-document duplication.
* **Commercial Invoice:** Primary accounting document detailing description, unit price, Incoterm, currency, harmonized code, and total invoice value.
* **Packing List:** Detailed breakdown of package numbers, gross/net weights, dimensions, and contents of each package.
* **Bill of Lading (B/L):** Three-fold legal character: 1. Receipt of cargo by ocean carrier, 2. Evidence of contract of carriage, 3. Document of title to goods. Sub-types: Clean, Claused/Foul, Shipped on Board, Received for Shipment, Charter Party, House B/L, Multimodal/Through B/L.
* **Air Waybill (AWB):** Contract of carriage and receipt of goods by air carrier; **NOT a document of title** (non-negotiable).
* **Shipping Bill (SB):** Statutory export declaration filed electronically via ICEGATE with Customs under Section 50 of the Customs Act, 1962 (Types: Duty Free, Dutiable, Drawback, Export under Bond/LUT).
* **Certificate of Origin (CoO):** Preferential CoO (for FTA/PTA concessions, e.g., SAFTA, ASEAN-India) vs Non-Preferential CoO (issued by Chambers of Commerce / trade bodies).
* **Bill of Exchange (Draft):** Unconditional written order signed by drawer (exporter) directing drawee (importer/bank) to pay on demand (Sight Draft) or at a determinable future date (Usance Draft) under Negotiable Instruments Act 1881.
* **e-BRC (Electronic Bank Realization Certificate):** Digital transmission from AD bank to DGFT server confirming realization of foreign currency against specific Shipping Bill.

## UNIT II: Regulatory Framework, Contracts, Payments, Incoterms, Logistics & EDI

### 1. Regulatory Framework
* **Foreign Trade (Development and Regulation) Act, 1992:** Replaced the rigid Import and Export (Control) Act 1947, transforming government's role from regulator to trade facilitator.
* **Foreign Trade Policy (FTP 2023):** Administered by DGFT; four pillars: Incentive to Remission, Export promotion through Collaboration, Ease of Doing Business, Emerging Areas (E-Commerce, SCOMET).
* **Negative List of Exports/Imports:** Goods classified into Prohibited (banned), Restricted (subject to specific DGFT license), and Canalised (imported/exported only through designated State Trading Enterprises like STC/MMTC).
* **SCOMET:** Special Chemicals, Organisms, Materials, Equipment and Technologies (dual-use items requiring strict export authorization).
* **FEMA 1999 & Current Account Convertibility:** Rupee is fully convertible on current account transactions (including exports/imports of goods and services).

### 2. Export Sales Contract
* **Vienna Convention (CISG 1980):** UN Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods; provides uniform international sales law.
* **Inquiry $\rightarrow$ Quotation / Proforma Invoice $\rightarrow$ Purchase Order $\rightarrow$ Order Acceptance:** Four-stage contractual evolution.
* **Essential Clauses:** Preamble, Specification, Inspection, Incoterm 2020, Payment mechanism, Passing of title, Liquidated damages, Force Majeure, Governing Law, and ICC Arbitration.
* **Force Majeure Clause:** Excuses non-performance due to unforeseeable, unavoidable, and external events (natural disasters, war, government embargoes).

### 3. Methods of Payment & Letters of Credit
* **Cash in Advance:** 100% risk on buyer; zero risk for exporter.
* **Open Account:** 100% risk on exporter; goods shipped before payment; common in intra-group MNC trade or high-trust relationships.
* **Documentary Collection (URC 522):** Document against Payment (D/P or Sight Draft) vs Document against Acceptance (D/A or Usance Draft); banks act as collecting intermediaries without payment obligation.
* **Letter of Credit (LC / Documentary Credit - UCP 600):** Irrevocable written undertaking by an Issuing Bank to pay the beneficiary (exporter) against presentation of strictly conforming documents.
* **Doctrine of Strict Compliance:** Documents must strictly adhere to the terms and conditions of the credit; minor typographic deviations justify bank rejection.
* **Doctrine of Independence (UCP 600 Art. 4):** LC is a separate transaction from the underlying commercial sales contract; banks deal in documents, not goods (Art. 5).
* **LC Varieties:** Revocable/Irrevocable, Confirmed/Unconfirmed, Sight/Usance, Transferable, Back-to-Back, Standby LC (ISP98), Revolving, Red Clause (pre-shipment advance), Green Clause (advance + warehousing).

### 4. Incoterms® 2020 (ICC Publication No. 723E)
* **11 Standard Rules:** Divided into Multimodal (EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP) and Maritime Only (FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF).
* **EXW (Ex Works):** Minimum seller obligation; buyer arranges carriage, insurance, and export customs clearance.
* **FCA (Free Carrier):** Flexible; seller clears goods for export and hands over to carrier nominated by buyer at seller’s premises or named terminal.
* **FOB (Free on Board):** Risk transfers when cargo is safely loaded on board the vessel nominated by buyer at the named port of shipment.
* **CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight):** Seller pays sea freight and procures minimum insurance cover (ICC C); risk transfers at the load port when loaded on board.
* **CIP (Carriage and Insurance Paid to):** Multimodal; seller pays carriage and must procure maximum insurance cover (ICC A) under Incoterms 2020.
* **DPU (Delivered at Place Unloaded):** Only Incoterm where seller is obligated to unload cargo at the destination terminal/place.
* **DDP (Delivered Duty Paid):** Maximum seller obligation; seller bears all costs, risks, import customs clearance, and import duties/taxes at destination.

### 5. Shipment, Customs Clearance & EDI
* **Stuffing:** Loading cargo into ocean containers (Factory stuffing vs CFS stuffing).
* **FCL vs LCL:** Full Container Load (single shipper/consignee) vs Less than Container Load (cargo consolidated at CFS from multiple shippers).
* **CFS & ICD:** Container Freight Station (maritime port extension) and Inland Container Depot (dry port in hinterland providing multimodal customs clearance).
* **Carting Order:** Port authority permission allowing exporter's cargo to enter the port/transit shed.
* **Let Export Order (LEO):** Statutory order issued by Customs Appraiser under Section 51 of Customs Act 1962 permitting cargo to be laden on board.
* **Out of Charge (OOC):** Statutory order issued under Section 47 of Customs Act 1962 releasing imported goods for home consumption.
* **ICES & ICEGATE:** Indian Customs Electronic Data Interchange System; Indian Customs EDI Gateway (e-filing portal for Shipping Bills, Bills of Entry, e-Sanchit, e-Payment).
* **Risk Management System (RMS):** Intelligent automated green-channel clearance module evaluating cargo risk to facilitate clearance without assessment/examination.
* **Single Window Interface for Facilitating Trade (SWIFT):** Integrated platform allowing importers/exporters to file a single Integrated Declaration, coordinating 6 participating government agencies (PGAs like FSSAI, Plant Quarantine, Wildlife).

## UNIT III: Finance, Banking, Factoring, Insurance, Risks, Inspection & HS

### 1. Pre-Shipment Finance (Packing Credit)
* **Pre-Shipment Packing Credit:** Concessional working capital finance extended by commercial banks to purchase raw materials, process, manufacture, pack, and transport export cargo.
* **Rupee Packing Credit vs PCFC (Packing Credit in Foreign Currency):** Rupee advance at domestic benchmark vs foreign currency loan at SOFR-linked international interest rates.
* **Eligibility Prerequisites:** Valid IEC, export order / confirmed LC, RCMC, not on RBI/ECGC caution/specific approval list.
* **Liquidation of Packing Credit:** Mandatory liquidation within stipulated period (normally up to 180 days) out of export proceeds, post-shipment export credit, or discounting of export bills.

### 2. Role of Commercial Banks
* **Authorized Dealer (AD) Category-I Banks:** Scheduled commercial banks authorized by RBI to deal in foreign exchange under Section 10 of FEMA 1999.
* **Advising Bank:** Bank in exporter's country that verifies the apparent authenticity of the LC and forwards it to beneficiary without payment commitment.
* **Confirming Bank:** Bank that adds its independent irrevocable payment guarantee to the LC upon request of issuing bank (UCP 600 Art. 8).
* **Negotiating Bank:** Bank that purchases/discounts conforming drafts and documents presented by the beneficiary.
* **EDPMS & IDPMS:** Export Data Processing and Monitoring System; Import Data Processing and Monitoring System (RBI electronic monitoring databases).

### 3. Factoring & Forfaiting
* **Export Factoring:** Financial arrangement where a financial intermediary (Factor) purchases exporter's short-term trade receivables (open account / D/A terms), providing ledger administration, collection, and credit protection.
* **Two-Factor System:** Operating mechanism of Factors Chain International (FCI) involving Export Factor in seller's country and Import Factor in buyer's country.
* **Recourse vs Non-Recourse Factoring:** Factor bears 100% bad-debt credit risk under Non-Recourse; exporter reimburses factor if buyer defaults under Recourse.
* **Forfaiting:** Purchasing medium to long-term export receivables (promissory notes or usance bills of exchange guaranteed/avalised by an international bank) on a **100% non-recourse** basis at a discounted rate.

### 4. Marine Cargo Insurance & ECGC
* **Marine Insurance Act, 1963:** Governing Indian statute based on the UK Marine Insurance Act 1906.
* **Core Principles:** Utmost Good Faith (*Uberrimae Fidei*), Insurable Interest, Indemnity, Subrogation, Proximate Cause (*Causa Proxima*), Contribution.
* **Institute Cargo Clauses (ICC 2009):**
  * **ICC (C):** Minimum cover; major casualties (fire, explosion, stranding, sinking, overturning, collision, discharge at port of distress, general average sacrifice).
  * **ICC (B):** Intermediate cover; adds earthquake, volcanic eruption, lightning, washing overboard, entry of sea/lake/river water, total loss of sling/package dropped during loading/unloading.
  * **ICC (A):** "All Risks" cover; covers all fortuitous loss/damage subject to standard exclusions (ordinary wear/tear, improper packing, inherent vice, insolvency of shipowners, willful misconduct).
* **General Average vs Particular Average:**
  * *General Average (GA):* Extraordinary voluntary sacrifice/expenditure intentionally made to preserve common maritime venture from peril; shared proportionally by ship, cargo, and freight (York-Antwerp Rules).
  * *Particular Average (PA):* Accidental, partial loss borne solely by the owner of the damaged property.
* **ECGC Standard Policy (SCR):** Shipments Comprehensive Risks policy covering commercial risks (buyer's insolvency, protracted default) and political risks (war, import ban, moratorium on foreign exchange remittance).

### 5. Quality Inspection & Harmonized System (HS)
* **Export (Quality Control and Inspection) Act, 1963:** Statutory legislation establishing Export Inspection Council (EIC) to ensure export consignments meet global standards.
* **Systems of Inspection:** 1. Consignment-wise Inspection (physical verification and sampling of each export lot), 2. In-Process Quality Control (IPQC - plant auditing), 3. Self-Certification Scheme (bestowed on industrial units with proven quality infrastructure).
* **Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS):** Universal 6-digit classification framework administered by World Customs Organization (WCO).
* **Indian ITC(HS):** 8-digit classification system:
  * Digits 1–2: **Chapter** (e.g., 61: Articles of apparel, knitted)
  * Digits 3–4: **Heading** (e.g., 61.09: T-shirts, singlets)
  * Digits 5–6: **Subheading** (e.g., 6109.10: Of cotton)
  * Digits 7–8: **Tariff Item** (National detailed classification in Indian Customs Tariff Act 1975).
* **General Rules of Interpretation (GRI Rules 1 to 6):** Hierarchical principles for resolving classification ambiguities (Rule 1: Terms of headings and section/chapter notes; Rule 3a: Most specific description; Rule 3b: Essential character).
