### 2. Statutory Turnaround Times & Legal Thresholds

* **Export Proceeds Realization (FEMA 1999):**
  * Normal exports: **9 months** from the date of export (as per RBI Master Direction on Export of Goods and Services).
  * Status Holder exporters / SEZ units / exports to warehouse outside India: Extended periods as sanctioned by RBI (normally 9 months, up to 15 months for overseas warehouse sales).
  * Period during COVID disruptions: Temporarily raised to 15 months, now restored to 9 months.
* **Document Examination under Letter of Credit (UCP 600 Article 14b):**
  * Maximum of **5 banking days** following the day of presentation to determine compliance.
* **Document Presentation Period under UCP 600 (Article 14c):**
  * Within **21 calendar days** after the date of shipment, but in any event not later than the expiry date of the credit (unless otherwise stipulated in the LC).
* **Filing of Bill of Entry (Section 46 of Customs Act 1962):**
  * Must be presented electronically **before the end of the day preceding the day** (including holidays) on which the aircraft/vessel/vehicle carrying the goods arrives at the customs station. Late fee applies for delayed filing.
* **Payment of Customs Import Duty (Section 47 of Customs Act 1962):**
  * Importer must pay duty **on the same day** or within the specified business day of electronic return of the assessed Bill of Entry; interest accrues thereafter.
* **Pre-Shipment Packing Credit Period (RBI Directions):**
  * Concessional interest rate typically available for up to **180 days** (extendable to a maximum of 270 or 360 days subject to bank discretion and interest rate escalation).
* **HS Code Structure:**
  * WCO Standard: **6 digits** (Universal across all WTO/WCO members).
  * Indian ITC(HS) National Code: **8 digits** (Tariff Item level).

### 3. Historical Syllabus Conventions vs Current Indian Ground Reality

Examiners who have been teaching for 15+ years may formulate questions using legacy terms from older editions of the textbooks. In your exam answers, **always present the traditional textbook position first, followed immediately by the current post-reform position.** This satisfies both traditional and modern examiners.

#### A. Central Excise Clearance vs GST Treatment
* **Traditional Textbook Position (Pre-July 2017):**
  * Governed by Central Excise Act, 1944.
  * Goods cleared from factory under **Form ARE-1** (Application for Removal of Excisable Goods for Export).
  * Exporter chose between two routes:
    1. *Rebate of Duty (Rule 18):* Pay excise duty at factory removal, export goods, and claim rebate of duty paid from Maritime Commissioner.
    2. *Export Under Bond / Letter of Undertaking (Rule 19):* Execute B-1 Bond with bank guarantee or LUT, procure duty-free inputs using Form CT-1, and export without paying central excise duty.
* **Present Legal Position (Post-GST, 2017 onwards):**
  * Central Excise subsumed under GST (except petroleum and liquor for human consumption).
  * Exports are classified as **"Zero Rated Supplies"** under Section 16 of the Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST) Act, 2017.
  * The two routes under GST are:
    1. *Export under Letter of Undertaking (LUT) / Bond:* Without payment of IGST; exporter accumulates input tax credit (ITC) and files for an ITC refund via Form GST RFD-01A.
    2. *Export on Payment of IGST:* Pay IGST at the time of export and claim an automatic electronic refund directly credited via ICEGATE linked to the Shipping Bill.
  * **ARE-1 form is obsolete** (except for petroleum products). The electronic Shipping Bill serves as the refund application.

#### B. Exchange Control Forms: GR / SDF vs EDPMS / e-BRC
* **Traditional Textbook Position:**
  * To monitor foreign exchange realization, the exporter had to file physical **GR (Guaranteed Realization) Form** in duplicate at the customs port, or **SDF (Statutory Declaration Form)** attached to the shipping bill under early EDI.
  * Payment realization was certified by the commercial bank via physical **Bank Realization Certificate (BRC)** on bank stationery with security seals.
* **Present Legal Position:**
  * Physical GR/SDF forms were completely abolished by RBI.
  * Customs electronically pushes export data from ICEGATE directly to RBI’s centralized **EDPMS (Export Data Processing and Monitoring System)**.
  * Authorized Dealer (AD) Category-I banks reconcile inward remittances directly against shipping bill numbers in EDPMS.
  * Banks generate an electronic **e-BRC**, which is digitally transmitted directly to the DGFT server for automatic claim of export promotion incentives (like RoDTEP, RoSCTL, Advance Authorization).

#### C. Foreign Trade Policy Evolution
* **Traditional Textbook Position:**
  * Refers to 5-year fixed duration policies (e.g., FTP 2004–2009, FTP 2009–2014, FTP 2015–2020).
  * Major incentive schemes were MEIS (Merchandise Exports from India Scheme) and SEIS (Service Exports from India Scheme).
* **Present Legal Position:**
  * **Foreign Trade Policy 2023** (effective from April 1, 2023) has **no fixed end date** (dynamic, living policy framework).
  * Target: Achieving **$2 Trillion in Indian exports** (Goods & Services) by 2030.
  * MEIS was withdrawn (due to WTO dispute panel ruling on export subsidies under ASCM) and replaced by WTO-compliant duty remission schemes: **RoDTEP (Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products)** and **RoSCTL (Remission of State and Central Taxes and Levies)**.

#### D. Incoterms Evolution: 2000 vs 2010 vs 2020
* **Traditional Misconceptions:**
  * "Risk passes when the goods cross the ship's rail under FOB/CFR/CIF." $\rightarrow$ **FALSE.** The "ship's rail" concept was eliminated in Incoterms 2010. Under Incoterms 2020, risk passes when goods are **safely loaded on board the vessel**.
  * Incoterms 2020 replaced **DAT (Delivered at Terminal)** with **DPU (Delivered at Place Unloaded)** to clarify that delivery can take place anywhere, not just at a transport terminal.
  * Insurance cover divergence: **CIF** requires minimum cover ICC (C) (cargo), whereas **CIP** under Incoterms 2020 now requires maximum cover **ICC (A)** ("All Risks"), unless mutually agreed otherwise.

### 4. Items Tagged for Quick Review Before Entering Hall

1. **[VERIFY: Current RoDTEP rates for specific sectors]**: RoDTEP rates vary by tariff line (typically between 0.5% and 4.3%). In exam answers, cite: "Rates vary between 0.5% to 4% depending on the ITC(HS) 8-digit schedule notified by DGFT".
2. **[VERIFY: EIC Inspection Charges]**: Standard inspection fee is nominal (historically 0.2% to 0.4% of FOB value), but modern consignments increasingly operate under Self-Certification and Approved Exporter Schemes where no per-consignment fee is levied.
3. **[VERIFY: Status Holder Export Performance Thresholds in FTP 2023]**:
   * One Star: US $ 3 Million
   * Two Star: US $ 15 Million
   * Three Star: US $ 50 Million
   * Four Star: US $ 200 Million
   * Five Star: US $ 800 Million
   *(Note: Substantially rationalized downwards in FTP 2023 compared to FTP 2015-20 to allow more MSMEs to achieve Star Export House status).*
