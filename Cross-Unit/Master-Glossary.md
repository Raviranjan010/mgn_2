### A

1. **Advance Authorization:** A duty exemption scheme under India's Foreign Trade Policy allowing duty-free import of raw materials and physical inputs required for manufacturing export products. `[Unit II: Regulatory Framework]`
2. **Advance Payment:** A payment method where the foreign buyer transfers 100% of the purchase price to the exporter before goods are manufactured or dispatched; lowest risk for exporter, highest risk for buyer. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
3. **Advising Bank:** A bank operating in the exporter's country that receives a Letter of Credit from an overseas issuing bank, authenticates its electronic signatures, and delivers it to the exporter without assuming financial payment liability. `[Unit II: Payment Methods / Unit III: Role of Banks]`
4. **Agmark:** A statutory certification mark issued by India's Directorate of Marketing and Inspection (DMI) that grades agricultural and food produce (ghee, butter, pulses, spices, honey) according to national quality standards. `[Unit III: Quality Inspection]`
5. **Air Waybill (AWB):** A transport document issued by an air cargo carrier serving as a receipt for cargo and evidence of the contract of carriage; non-negotiable and strictly never a document of title. `[Unit I: Documentation]`
6. **Aligned Documentation System (ADS):** A standardized documentation framework based on the United Nations Layout Key (UNLK) where all commercial trade documents are formatted to uniform A4 dimensions, enabling automated printing from a master profile. `[Unit I: Documentation]`
7. **Amber Box Subsidies:** Domestic agricultural support measures under the WTO Agreement on Agriculture that are considered trade-distorting (e.g., Minimum Support Price - MSP, fertilizer subsidies); subject to monetary De Minimis caps. `[Unit I: WTO]`
8. **Anti-Dumping Duty (ADD):** A border tariff imposed by an importing government under GATT Article VI to protect domestic industries against foreign goods dumped at prices below their normal domestic market value. `[Unit I: WTO / Unit II: Regulatory]`
9. **APEDA:** Agricultural and Processed Food Products Export Development Authority; statutory body under the Ministry of Commerce responsible for export infrastructure, packhouse accreditation, and promotion of agro-processed foods. `[Unit I: Agencies]`
10. **Appraising Officer (Appraiser):** A specialized customs officer at ports who evaluates imported/exported goods for correct valuation, ITC(HS) classification, and tax assessment. `[Unit III: Clearance of Cargo]`
11. **Arbitration Clause:** A mandatory contractual clause stipulating that any cross-border commercial dispute will be resolved privately through an institutional arbitral tribunal (SIAC, ICC, ICA) rather than costly domestic courts. `[Unit II: Sales Contract]`
12. **ARE-1 (Application for Removal of Excisable Goods):** The traditional statutory central excise form used by Indian manufacturers to remove excisable finished goods from factories for export under claim for rebate. `[Unit II: Central Excise]`
13. **Article 14 (UCP 600):** Bedrock banking rule providing that banks have a maximum of 5 banking days following the day of presentation to examine documentary credit documents and determine compliance. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
14. **Article 4 (UCP 600) - Autonomy of Credit:** Bedrock banking rule stating that a Letter of Credit is legally separate and independent from the underlying commercial sales contract; banks deal exclusively with documents, not physical goods. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
15. **Authorized Dealer (AD Category-I):** A commercial bank licensed by the Reserve Bank of India under Section 10 of FEMA 1999 to buy, sell, and settle foreign exchange transactions for cross-border trade. `[Unit III: Role of Banks]`
16. **Authorized Economic Operator (AEO):** A trade facilitation program run by Indian Customs certifying high-compliance exporters and importers for expedited Green Channel clearance and reduced bank guarantees. `[Unit II: EDI]`
17. **Aval:** A specialized, irrevocable bank guarantee written directly onto the face of a cross-border Bill of Exchange or Promissory Note, legally obligating the guaranteeing bank to pay if the primary obligor defaults. `[Unit III: Factoring & Forfaiting]`

### B

18. **Back-to-Back Letter of Credit:** A secondary, independent Letter of Credit opened by an intermediary merchant exporter in favour of an actual domestic manufacturer, backed by the collateral of the primary LC opened by the overseas buyer. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
19. **Beneficiary:** The designated party in whose favour a Letter of Credit or financial guarantee is opened; in export transactions, the seller/exporter. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
20. **Bill of Entry (B/E):** The legal declaration filed electronically on ICEGATE by an importer or Customs Broker under Section 46 of the Customs Act, 1962, seeking customs clearance for inward cargo. `[Unit II: Shipment & Customs]`
21. **Bill of Exchange (Draft):** An unconditional written order signed by the drawer (exporter) directing the drawee (buyer/bank) to pay a specified sum of money at sight or on a fixed future date. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
22. **Bill of Lading (Ocean B/L):** The master transport document issued by an ocean carrier performing three concurrent legal functions: (1) receipt for loaded cargo, (2) transferable document of title, and (3) evidence of carriage contract. `[Unit I: Documentation]`
23. **Blue Box Subsidies:** Agricultural support payments under the WTO Agreement on Agriculture made directly to farmers under production-limiting programs; exempted from reduction commitments. `[Unit I: WTO]`
24. **Bonded Warehouse:** A customs-licensed, secured storage warehouse where imported dutiable goods may be stored under a yellow Bill of Entry with payment of customs duty deferred until final domestic release. `[Unit II: Shipment & Customs]`
25. **Bound Tariff:** The maximum customs duty ceiling agreed upon by a WTO member government during multilateral negotiations; applied tariff rates can be lower than bound rates, but never higher. `[Unit I: WTO]`
26. **BRC (Bank Realization Certificate):** A statutory certificate issued by an Authorized Dealer bank confirming that export proceeds have been successfully realized in foreign currency in India. `[Unit I: Steps / Unit III: Role of Banks]`
27. **Break Bulk Cargo:** Non-containerized general cargo packaged in bags, crates, drums, or pallets, loaded individually into the vessel's hold. `[Unit II: Shipment & Customs]`
28. **Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS):** The national standards body of India responsible for the formulation of product specifications and granting the mandatory ISI quality mark. `[Unit III: Quality Inspection]`
29. **Buyer's Credit:** A short-term loan extended by an overseas bank or financial institution to an Indian importer to pay for import shipments, based on a Letter of Comfort issued by the importer's domestic bank. `[Unit III: Role of Banks]`

### C

30. **Canalised Goods (State Trading Enterprises):** Commodities whose import or export is strictly restricted by government policy to designated state-owned trading corporations (MMTC, STC, FCI, IOCL). `[Unit II: Regulatory Framework]`
31. **Carriage and Insurance Paid To (CIP):** An Incoterms 2020 multimodal rule where the seller pays freight and maximum marine insurance cover (ICC A - All Risks) to the named foreign destination, while risk transfers at origin. `[Unit II: Incoterms]`
32. **Carriage Paid To (CPT):** An Incoterms 2020 multimodal rule where the seller pays freight to the named foreign destination, but risk transfers to the buyer upon delivering cargo to the first carrier at origin. `[Unit II: Incoterms]`
33. **Certificate of Inspection:** A statutory quality assurance document issued by the Export Inspection Agency (EIA) or designated private surveyor (SGS) certifying cargo meets mandated specifications. `[Unit I: Documentation / Unit III: Quality Inspection]`
34. **Certificate of Origin (COO):** An official document certifying the country in which the exported goods were grown, manufactured, or processed; essential for determining applicable tariff rates. `[Unit I: Documentation]`
35. **CFR (Cost and Freight):** An Incoterms 2020 maritime rule where the seller pays transport costs to the destination port, but cargo loss/damage risk transfers to the buyer once goods pass on board the ship at origin. `[Unit II: Incoterms]`
36. **CFS (Container Freight Station):** A customs-bonded inland transit facility located near maritime ports where ocean containers are packed (stuffed) for export or unpacked (destuffed) for import. `[Unit II: Shipment & Customs]`
37. **Charter Party Bill of Lading:** A Bill of Lading issued subject to the terms of a charter party agreement (vessel charter contract); generally unacceptable to banks under UCP 600 unless expressly authorized. `[Unit I: Documentation]`
38. **CIF (Cost, Insurance & Freight):** An Incoterms 2020 maritime rule where the seller pays freight and minimum marine insurance (ICC C) to the destination port, but cargo risk transfers to the buyer upon loading at origin. `[Unit II: Incoterms]`
39. **CIP vs CIF Divergence:** Under Incoterms 2020, CIP mandates high-level all-risks insurance (ICC A), whereas maritime CIF mandates basic minimum casualty insurance (ICC C). `[Unit II: Incoterms]`
40. **CISG:** United Nations Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods (Vienna 1980); uniform multilateral framework establishing binding rules for cross-border commercial sales. `[Unit II: Sales Contract]`
41. **Claused Bill of Lading (Foul / Dirty B/L):** A Bill of Lading containing an explicit written notation by the carrier recording damaged packaging, leaking containers, or defective external condition of goods. `[Unit I: Documentation]`
42. **Clean Bill of Lading:** A Bill of Lading bearing no adverse clauses or notations regarding the external condition or packaging of cargo at loading; required by banks under UCP 600 Art. 27. `[Unit I: Documentation]`
43. **Collecting Bank:** Under ICC URC 522, any bank (other than the remitting bank) involved in processing a documentary collection in the buyer's country. `[Unit II: Payment Methods / Unit III: Role of Banks]`
44. **Commercial Invoice:** The primary accounting and legal document in international trade itemizing the goods sold, price per unit, Incoterm, payment terms, and total financial transaction value. `[Unit I: Documentation]`
45. **Commodity Boards:** Statutory promotion bodies established by Parliament (Tea Board, Coffee Board, Spices Board, Rubber Board) dedicated to research, domestic output, and export development. `[Unit I: Agencies]`
46. **Confirmed Letter of Credit:** A Letter of Credit to which a secondary, reputable bank (confirming bank, usually in seller's country) adds its own independent irrevocable payment undertaking, eliminating foreign bank/country risk. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
47. **Consignment Sale:** A high-risk commercial payment arrangement where goods are dispatched to a foreign agent on trust, with payment remitted to the exporter only after goods are sold to end-users. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
48. **Consular Invoice:** A commercial invoice formalized and stamped by the consulate or embassy of the importing country located in the exporter's territory. `[Unit I: Documentation]`
49. **Countervailing Duty (CVD):** A targeted border tariff imposed under GATT Article VI / SCM Agreement to neutralize the artificial price advantage of foreign imported goods subsidized by their home governments. `[Unit I: WTO / Unit II: Regulatory]`
50. **Customs Broker (CHA):** A licensed professional authorized under the Customs Act to act as an agent on behalf of importers and exporters for processing customs declarations and cargo clearance. `[Unit II: Shipment & Customs]`

### D

51. **D/A (Documents Against Acceptance):** A documentary collection mechanism under URC 522 where the buyer's bank releases original title documents only after the buyer formally signs (accepts) a time/usance bill of exchange. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
52. **DAP (Delivered at Place):** An Incoterms 2020 multimodal rule where the seller delivers goods to the buyer's named destination place, ready for unloading by the buyer, bearing all transit risks up to that point. `[Unit II: Incoterms]`
53. **DDP (Delivered Duty Paid):** The Incoterms 2020 rule imposing maximum liability on the seller, who bears all transport costs, marine insurance, transit risks, and foreign customs import duties/taxes up to the buyer's door. `[Unit II: Incoterms]`
54. **De Minimis:** The legal threshold in the WTO Agreement on Agriculture permitting developing nations (like India) to provide trade-distorting domestic farm subsidies up to 10% of total agricultural output value. `[Unit I: WTO]`
55. **Demurrage:** Financial storage penalty charged by the seaport or terminal authority for containers or cargo remaining inside the customs terminal beyond the allotted free days. `[Unit II: Shipment & Customs]`
56. **Detention:** Equipment penalty charged by ocean container shipping lines when a container is held by an importer/exporter outside port premises beyond the contractually permitted free return days. `[Unit II: Shipment & Customs]`
57. **DGCIS:** Directorate General of Commercial Intelligence and Statistics (Kolkata); official government agency responsible for compiling and publishing India's national foreign trade and balance of payments statistics. `[Unit I: Agencies]`
58. **DGFT:** Directorate General of Foreign Trade (New Delhi); apex statutory body under the Ministry of Commerce responsible for formulating and administering India's Foreign Trade Policy (FTP) and issuing IECs. `[Unit I: Agencies / Unit II: Regulatory]`
59. **Discrepancy (in LC):** Any variance between documents presented by an exporter and the terms, conditions, and international banking rules (UCP 600) specified in the governing Letter of Credit. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
60. **Dispute Settlement Understanding (DSU):** The binding judicial dispute resolution annex of the WTO establishing procedures, panels, timelines, and the reverse consensus rule to settle trade disputes between member states. `[Unit I: WTO]`
61. **Doctor of Subrogation:** Common law marine insurance doctrine stating that once an insurer settles a total or partial loss claim, they step into the insured's legal shoes to recover damages from negligent third parties. `[Unit III: Insurance]`
62. **Document of Title:** A legally negotiable commercial document (e.g., Ocean Bill of Lading) whose physical possession and endorsement transfers lawful legal ownership of the goods described therein. `[Unit I: Documentation]`
63. **Documentary Collection:** A payment process governed by ICC URC 522 whereby the exporter instructs their domestic bank to forward commercial shipping documents to the buyer's bank for release against payment (D/P) or acceptance (D/A). `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
64. **DPU (Delivered at Place Unloaded):** An Incoterms 2020 rule (which replaced DAT) where the seller is contractually obligated to deliver and physically unload goods at the named foreign terminal/destination. `[Unit II: Incoterms]`
65. **Drawback (Duty Drawback):** Statutory refund under Sections 74 and 75 of the Customs Act, 1962, returning customs and excise duties paid on imported materials used in the manufacture of exported goods. `[Unit II: Regulatory Framework]`
66. **DSB (Dispute Settlement Body):** The full WTO General Council convening to administer dispute settlement rules, establish panels, adopt appellate rulings, and authorize retaliatory trade sanctions. `[Unit I: WTO]`
67. **Dual-Use Items:** Products, materials, software, or technologies that have legitimate commercial industrial uses but can also be adapted for weapons of mass destruction (regulated under SCOMET). `[Unit II: Regulatory Framework]`

### E

68. **e-BRC:** Electronic Bank Realization Certificate; a digital document electronically uploaded by Authorized Dealer banks to the DGFT central database confirming foreign exchange realization for an export order. `[Unit I: Steps / Unit III: Role of Banks]`
69. **ECGC:** Export Credit Guarantee Corporation of India; a state-owned export credit insurance agency protecting Indian exporters against commercial and political payment defaults by overseas buyers. `[Unit I: Agencies / Unit III: Insurance]`
70. **EDI (Electronic Data Interchange):** The structured computer-to-computer electronic transmission of standard business and customs trade documentation based on standardized data messaging formats (UN/EDIFACT). `[Unit II: EDI]`
71. **EDIFACT:** United Nations rules for Electronic Data Interchange for Administration, Commerce and Transport; the globally recognized electronic messaging syntax standard for international customs declarations. `[Unit II: EDI]`
72. **EDPMS:** Export Data Processing and Monitoring System; real-time web portal managed by the Reserve Bank of India to automatically monitor, reconcile, and close export shipping bills against bank inward remittances. `[Unit I: Steps / Unit III: Role of Banks]`
73. **EIA (Export Inspection Agency):** The field operational network of five regional government inspection bodies (with 40+ maritime sub-offices) operating under the Export Inspection Council to inspect and certify export cargo. `[Unit III: Quality Inspection]`
74. **EIC (Export Inspection Council):** Apex statutory body established under the Export (Quality Control and Inspection) Act, 1963, to advise the Indian government and enforce quality standards on notified export goods. `[Unit I: Agencies / Unit III: Quality Inspection]`
75. **Electronic Cash Ledger (ECL):** An online wallet facility on ICEGATE allowing importers and customs brokers to deposit funds in advance for instant electronic settlement of customs duties. `[Unit II: EDI]`
76. **EPC (Export Promotion Council):** Non-profit sector-specific promotional associations (e.g., EEPC, AEPC) established to promote sectoral export growth, market research, and issue statutory RCMCs to exporters. `[Unit I: Agencies]`
77. **EPCG (Export Promotion Capital Goods) Scheme:** An Indian FTP trade scheme enabling manufacturers to import capital machinery at zero customs duty subject to fulfilling an export obligation equal to 6 times the duty saved. `[Unit II: Regulatory Framework]`
78. **Essential Character Rule (GRI 3b):** Harmonized System classification principle stating that composite goods or retail assortments that cannot be classified by specific description must be classified by the component that gives them their essential character. `[Unit III: Harmonized System]`
79. **Exchange Earners' Foreign Currency (EEFC) Account:** A special non-interest-bearing bank account maintained in foreign currency with an Indian AD bank allowing exporters to retain 100% of export earnings for authorized overseas expenses. `[Unit III: Pre-Shipment Finance]`
80. **EXIM Bank of India:** Export-Import Bank of India; apex statutory export financing institution established in 1982 to advance long-term trade finance, buyer's credit, and sovereign overseas Lines of Credit. `[Unit I: Agencies]`
81. **Export Duty:** A customs tariff levied by the central government on specific departing export goods (e.g., iron ore, raw hides, unmanufactured tobacco) to conserve domestic resources or curb price inflation. `[Unit II: Regulatory Framework]`
82. **Export Factor:** In a two-factor factoring arrangement, the financial factor located in the exporter's country that advances short-term cash, maintains ledgers, and interfaces with the export seller. `[Unit III: Factoring & Forfaiting]`
83. **EXW (Ex Works):** The Incoterms 2020 rule imposing minimum obligation on the seller, who merely makes goods available at their factory or warehouse; buyer bears all loading, transport, export, and import costs. `[Unit II: Incoterms]`

### F

84. **Factoring:** A financial service whereby a business sells its short-term trade receivables (invoices on open account or D/A terms) to a specialized commercial financial institution (Factor) at a discount for immediate cash. `[Unit III: Factoring & Forfaiting]`
85. **FAS (Free Alongside Ship):** An Incoterms 2020 maritime rule where the seller completes delivery when goods are placed physically alongside the vessel (on a quay or barge) at the named port of shipment. `[Unit II: Incoterms]`
86. **FCA (Free Carrier):** An Incoterms 2020 multimodal rule where the seller delivers goods to the carrier or nominated depot named by the buyer, cleared for export; ideal modern replacement for maritime FOB on container shipments. `[Unit II: Incoterms]`
87. **FCL (Full Container Load):** An ocean container shipment where an individual exporter rents an entire sealed shipping container for their own cargo exclusively, loaded at the factory or container freight station. `[Unit II: Shipment & Customs]`
88. **FCI (Factors Chain International):** The global umbrella association of international factoring companies establishing uniform operating rules (General Rules for International Factoring - GRIF) and mutual cross-border credit guarantees. `[Unit III: Factoring & Forfaiting]`
89. **FEMA (Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999):** Apex Indian statute regulating cross-border foreign exchange transactions, external payments, and statutory time limits for realizing export proceeds. `[Unit II: Regulatory Framework / Unit III: Banks]`
90. **FIEO (Federation of Indian Export Organisations):** The apex coordinating trade promotion body set up by the Ministry of Commerce representing all Indian export promotion councils, multi-product houses, and service exporters. `[Unit I: Agencies]`
91. **FIRC (Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate):** A formal document issued by Authorized Dealer commercial banks certifying receipt of inward foreign currency remittances from abroad into an Indian bank account. `[Unit III: Role of Banks]`
92. **FOB (Free On Board):** The classic Incoterms 2020 maritime rule where the seller bears all costs and risks until cargo is loaded on board the vessel at the named origin port; strictly prohibited for air/container multimodal transport. `[Unit II: Incoterms]`
93. **Force Majeure:** A standard contract clause relieving both parties from performance obligations when extraordinary, unforeseeable events beyond human control (war, earthquake, hurricane, government embargo) prevent execution. `[Unit II: Sales Contract]`
94. **Forfaiting:** A specialized non-recourse export financing mechanism whereby an exporter surrenders medium-to-long term foreign trade debt instruments (promissory notes/bills of exchange with bank Aval) to a Forfaiter for 100% immediate discounted cash. `[Unit III: Factoring & Forfaiting]`
95. **Forward Contract:** A binding financial derivative contract signed with an Authorized Dealer bank agreeing to sell or buy a specified amount of foreign currency at a fixed exchange rate on an agreed future maturity date. `[Unit III: Types of Risks / Banks]`
96. **Free Trade Agreement (FTA):** A preferential bilateral or multilateral treaty between nations eliminating border customs tariffs and non-tariff barriers on substantially all goods traded between signatories (GATT Art. XXIV). `[Unit I: WTO]`
97. **Fumigation Certificate:** A mandatory auxiliary quarantine certificate issued by accredited pest control agencies certifying timber packaging materials or agricultural lots have been treated with fumigants (Methyl Bromide). `[Unit I: Documentation]`

### G

98. **GATS:** General Agreement on Trade in Services; multilateral WTO treaty governing international trade in commercial services across 4 operational modes of supply. `[Unit I: WTO]`
99. **GATT:** General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (1947); the original multilateral trade treaty that reduced global border tariffs, later subsumed into the WTO framework in 1995. `[Unit I: WTO]`
100. **General Average:** A ancient maritime law principle where an extraordinary, voluntary sacrifice or expenditure is reasonably made in a moment of mortal maritime peril to save the common voyage (e.g., jettisoning cargo); shared proportionately by ship, cargo, and freight. `[Unit III: Insurance]`
101. **General Rules of Interpretation (GRI):** Six mandatory legal interpretation principles (Rules 1 to 6) formulated by the WCO governing the sequential classification of merchandise under the Harmonized System. `[Unit III: Harmonized System]`
102. **Green Box Subsidies:** Agricultural support payments under the WTO Agreement on Agriculture that cause minimal or no trade distortion (government R&D, disaster compensation, rural infrastructure); exempted from monetary caps. `[Unit I: WTO]`
103. **Green Channel Clearance:** An expedited, paperless customs clearance mechanism operated via the Risk Management System (RMS) allowing compliant consignments to clear without physical inspection or routine appraiser assessment. `[Unit II: EDI]`
104. **GSP (Generalized System of Preferences):** A non-reciprocal preferential tariff system permitted under GATT enabling developed economies to grant lower or zero tariff rates on manufactured exports from developing nations. `[Unit I: WTO]`

### H

105. **HACCP (Hazard Analysis Critical Control Point):** An internationally recognized preventative food safety management system that monitors chemical, biological, and physical hazards; mandatory for Indian seafood exports. `[Unit III: Quality Inspection]`
106. **Handbook of Procedures (HBP):** The operational administrative volume issued by DGFT detailing the procedural guidelines, formats, application forms, and documentation required to execute the Foreign Trade Policy. `[Unit II: Regulatory Framework]`
107. **Harmonized System (HS):** The universal international standardized 6-digit classification nomenclature developed by the World Customs Organization (WCO) for grouping and taxing all internationally traded physical commodities. `[Unit III: Harmonized System]`
108. **House Bill of Lading (HBL):** A transport document issued by an ocean freight forwarder or non-vessel operating common carrier (NVOCC) to an individual small shipper whose cargo has been consolidated with other lots. `[Unit I: Documentation]`

### I

109. **ICC (A) - All Risks:** The widest marine cargo insurance clause covering all risks of physical loss or damage to cargo during international transit, subject to statutory general exclusions (Inherent Vice, packing defects). `[Unit III: Insurance]`
110. **ICC (B):** An intermediate marine cargo insurance clause covering major maritime casualties plus specific named natural perils (earthquake, volcanic eruption, lightning, water entry, washing overboard). `[Unit III: Insurance]`
111. **ICC (C):** The most restricted, minimum marine cargo insurance clause covering only major catastrophic maritime events (fire, explosion, sinking, stranding, collision, capsizing, jettison). `[Unit III: Insurance]`
112. **ICD (Inland Container Depot):** An "inland dry port" equipped with customs assessment, cargo inspection, and container handling facilities, functioning as an independent customs station away from maritime coasts. `[Unit II: Shipment & Customs]`
113. **ICES (Indian Customs EDI System):** The centralized internal automated software engine utilized by Indian Customs officers across sea, air, and dry ports for computerized document processing, appraisal, and revenue collection. `[Unit II: EDI]`
114. **ICEGATE:** Indian Customs Electronic Gateway (`icegate.gov.in`); the national web portal enabling trade entities to submit electronic Shipping Bills, Bills of Entry, and payment of customs duties online. `[Unit II: EDI]`
115. **IDPMS:** Import Data Processing and Monitoring System; real-time Reserve Bank of India surveillance portal tracking cross-border foreign exchange payments made by Indian importers against physical customs arrivals. `[Unit III: Role of Banks]`
116. **IEC (Importer-Exporter Code):** A mandatory 10-digit PAN-based business identification number issued online by DGFT, compulsory for conducting commercial export or import operations in India. `[Unit I: Steps / Unit II: Regulatory]`
117. **IES (Interest Equalisation Scheme):** A central government trade incentive providing a 2% to 3% pre- and post-shipment rupee export credit interest subvention to MSME and eligible sectoral exporters. `[Unit III: Pre-Shipment Finance]`
118. **IGM (Import General Manifest):** A statutory legal manifest filed electronically by an inbound ocean or air carrier with customs under Section 30 of the Customs Act prior to vessel arrival, detailing all cargo on board. `[Unit II: Shipment & Customs]`
119. **IGST (Integrated Goods and Services Tax):** Tax levied under the IGST Act, 2017 on interstate supplies and imports; exports are designated as zero-rated supplies with options for LUT export or IGST refund. `[Unit II: Central Excise / Regulatory]`
120. **IIP (Indian Institute of Packaging):** An autonomous packaging research and educational body under the Ministry of Commerce that formulates export packaging standards and conducts mandatory UN testing for hazardous cargo. `[Unit I: Agencies]`
121. **Import Factor:** In a cross-border two-factor factoring mechanism, the financial factor located in the overseas buyer's country that evaluates buyer credit limits, recovers local debts, and guarantees payment. `[Unit III: Factoring & Forfaiting]`
122. **Incoterms 2020:** International Commercial Terms; a standardized set of 11 commercial rules published by the International Chamber of Commerce defining the allocation of costs, risks, and document responsibilities between buyer and seller. `[Unit II: Incoterms]`
123. **Indemnity (Principle of):** Fundamental insurance doctrine establishing that an insurance policy serves to restore the insured to their exact pre-loss financial position, preventing the policyholder from profiting from a loss. `[Unit III: Insurance]`
124. **Inherent Vice:** The natural tendency of certain physical goods to rot, spoil, ferment, evaporate, or combust spontaneously without any external peril; universally excluded from marine insurance coverage. `[Unit III: Insurance]`
125. **In-Process Quality Control (IPQC):** A statutory pre-shipment inspection methodology where quality checks are conducted continuously during manufacturing phases at the plant, rather than solely inspecting finished packaged cargo. `[Unit III: Quality Inspection]`
126. **Institute Cargo Clauses (ICC):** Standardized marine insurance contract clauses drafted and updated by the Institute of London Underwriters (IUA) and the Lloyd's Market Association, recognized globally. `[Unit III: Insurance]`
127. **Insurable Interest:** The legal and financial relationship between an insured party and the cargo such that the party suffers direct financial loss if the cargo is damaged or lost; required at time of loss in marine policies. `[Unit III: Insurance]`
128. **Irrevocable Letter of Credit:** A Letter of Credit that cannot be amended, altered, or cancelled by the issuing bank or buyer without the explicit, formal agreement of all parties, including the exporter (beneficiary). `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
129. **ISO 9001:** An international quality management system (QMS) standard published by the International Organization for Standardization certifying consistent organizational processes and customer satisfaction. `[Unit III: Quality Inspection]`
130. **ITC(HS):** Indian Trade Clarification based on the Harmonized System; the national 8-digit tariff nomenclature used by Indian Customs and DGFT for regulating and taxing cross-border merchandise. `[Unit II: Regulatory / Unit III: HS]`
131. **ITPO (India Trade Promotion Organisation):** The premier trade fair and trade promotion enterprise of the Ministry of Commerce managing Pragati Maidan exhibition facilities and international trade expos. `[Unit I: Agencies]`

### J

132. **Jettison:** The intentional, voluntary casting overboard of cargo, equipment, or ship parts during a maritime emergency to lighten a stranded or endangered vessel; classical act of General Average. `[Unit III: Insurance]`
133. **Joint Venture (in International Trade):** A cross-border commercial partnership where domestic exporters collaborate with overseas corporations, sharing equity, management, risks, and export market networks. `[Unit I: Steps]`

### L

134. **LCL (Less than Container Load):** A containerized shipping arrangement where cargo from multiple different small exporters is consolidated into a single shared container at a Container Freight Station (CFS). `[Unit II: Shipment & Customs]`
135. **LEO (Let Export Order):** The final statutory order issued by a Customs Officer under Section 51 of the Customs Act, 1962, certifying that export cargo has been fully inspected, assessed, and cleared for vessel loading. `[Unit II: Shipment & Customs]`
136. **Letter of Credit (LC):** A binding documentary payment undertaking issued by an issuing bank at the request of an applicant buyer, guaranteeing payment to the beneficiary exporter against presentation of strictly compliant documents. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
137. **Letter of Undertaking (LUT):** A statutory legal bond executed online by an Indian exporter on the GST portal enabling the export of goods or services without paying upfront IGST. `[Unit II: Central Excise / Regulatory]`
138. **Lighterage:** The commercial process of transferring cargo between ocean vessels and land quays using small barges (lighters) at shallow maritime ports where large ships cannot berth alongside quays. `[Unit II: Incoterms]`
139. **Line of Credit (LoC):** A sovereign trade finance facility extended by EXIM Bank of India to foreign overseas governments, funding their public infrastructure procurement from Indian manufacturers. `[Unit I: Agencies]`

### M

140. **Marine Insurance:** A contract of indemnity whereby the marine underwriter agrees to compensate the insured against losses incident to marine adventure, covering ocean, inland waterway, rail, road, and air transit risks. `[Unit III: Insurance]`
141. **Marrakesh Agreement:** The historic international trade agreement signed in Morocco in April 1994 concluding the Uruguay Round negotiations and establishing the World Trade Organization on 1 January 1995. `[Unit I: WTO]`
142. **Mate's Receipt:** A receipt issued by the Chief Mate (First Officer) of an ocean cargo ship acknowledging that cargo has been physically loaded on board the vessel, later exchanged for the signed Ocean Bill of Lading. `[Unit I: Documentation / Unit II: Shipment]`
143. **MFN (Most-Favoured-Nation):** Bedrock multilateral trade rule under GATT Article I prohibiting geographical discrimination; requires any trade concession granted to one country to be unconditionally granted to all WTO members. `[Unit I: WTO]`
144. **MMTC:** Metals and Minerals Trading Corporation of India; a premier state-owned canalised trading enterprise dealing in large-scale imports of gold bullion and exports of minerals and fertilizers. `[Unit I: Agencies]`
145. **MPEDA:** Marine Products Export Development Authority (Kochi); statutory agency under the Ministry of Commerce responsible for developing India's seafood industry and enforcing sanitary standards on fisheries exports. `[Unit I: Agencies]`

### N

146. **National Treatment:** Bedrock multilateral trade principle under GATT Article III requiring that once imported foreign goods have cleared customs and paid border tariffs, they must receive treatment no less favourable than domestic goods. `[Unit I: WTO]`
147. **Negative Consensus (Reverse Consensus):** DSU voting rule where a panel report or appellate decision is automatically adopted by the WTO Dispute Settlement Body unless *every single member state present* votes unanimously against it. `[Unit I: WTO]`
148. **Negative List:** A consolidated schedule published in the Foreign Trade Policy classifying specific restricted, prohibited, or state-canalised items requiring special government licensing. `[Unit II: Regulatory Framework]`
149. **Negotiating Bank:** A nominated commercial bank in the exporter's country that examines shipping documents presented under a freely negotiable LC and advances funds to the exporter before reimbursement from the issuing bank. `[Unit II: Payment Methods / Unit III: Banks]`
150. **Non-Recourse Factoring:** A factoring agreement where the Factor assumes 100% of the financial credit risk; if the overseas buyer becomes insolvent, the Factor absorbs the loss and cannot recover funds from the exporter. `[Unit III: Factoring & Forfaiting]`
151. **Non-Tariff Barriers (NTBs):** Administrative, regulatory, or technical measures other than ordinary customs tariffs that restrict cross-border trade (e.g., import quotas, complex packaging mandates, arbitrary SPS rules). `[Unit I: WTO]`
152. **NVOCC (Non-Vessel Operating Common Carrier):** A maritime freight consolidator who issues their own House Bills of Lading to shippers and assumes carrier liability without owning or operating ocean vessels. `[Unit I: Documentation]`

### O

153. **On-Board Bill of Lading:** A Bill of Lading containing a clear notation signed or initialed by the carrier certifying that the cargo has been physically loaded onto the named vessel; required under UCP 600 Art. 20. `[Unit I: Documentation]`
154. **OOC (Out of Charge):** The final statutory order issued by a Customs Officer under Section 47 of the Customs Act, 1962, certifying import duty has been paid and authorizing port custodians to release imported goods. `[Unit II: Shipment & Customs]`
155. **Open Account:** A commercial payment term where goods are produced, shipped, and delivered to the foreign buyer before payment is due, with payment remitted after an agreed credit duration (30–90 days). `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
156. **Open Cover (Marine Policy):** A continuous, open-ended marine insurance contract covering all international cargo shipments dispatched by an active exporter over a 12-month period against pre-agreed conditions and rates. `[Unit III: Insurance]`

### P

157. **Packing Credit:** Pre-shipment working capital bank finance extended at concessional interest rates to eligible exporters for procuring raw materials, manufacturing, packaging, and transporting goods up to the port. `[Unit III: Pre-Shipment Finance]`
158. **Packing List:** A vital shipping document accompanying the commercial invoice that details the specific physical contents, package counts, box dimensions, gross and net weights, and container numbers of a shipment. `[Unit I: Documentation]`
159. **Particular Average:** A accidental, fortuitous partial loss or damage caused by an insured peril affecting solely a specific cargo consignment, borne exclusively by that specific cargo owner or their insurer. `[Unit III: Insurance]`
160. **PCFC (Packing Credit in Foreign Currency):** Pre-shipment working capital loan sanctioned to Indian exporters in internationally convertible currencies (USD, EUR) linked to global benchmark interest rates (SOFR), providing a natural hedge. `[Unit III: Pre-Shipment Finance]`
161. **Phytosanitary Certificate:** A statutory quarantine certificate issued by the national plant protection organization certifying plant products, timber, or agricultural crops are free from quarantine pests and plant diseases. `[Unit I: Documentation]`
162. **Political Risk:** The risk that cross-border trade payments or goods will be obstructed or confiscated due to sovereign government actions (war, civil revolution, currency transfer bans, trade embargoes). `[Unit III: Types of Risks / Insurance]`
163. **Port of Discharge (POD):** The maritime port where cargo is physically unloaded from the carrying vessel in the importing country. `[Unit I: Documentation / Unit II: Incoterms]`
164. **Port of Loading (POL):** The maritime port where cargo is physically hoisted and secured on board the ocean vessel in the exporting country. `[Unit I: Documentation / Unit II: Incoterms]`
165. **Post-Shipment Finance:** Working capital credit facilities provided by Authorized Dealer banks to exporters after goods have been shipped, bridging the financial gap between shipment and payment realization. `[Unit III: Pre-Shipment Finance]`
166. **Preventive Officer:** A customs officer stationed at port gates, vessel berths, and container docks who physically verifies container seals, supervises vessel loading/unloading, and prevents smuggling. `[Unit III: Clearance of Cargo]`
167. **Proforma Invoice:** An initial estimated quotation document sent by an exporter to an overseas buyer detailing product specifications, price, Incoterm, and payment terms, serving as the basis for opening an LC. `[Unit I: Steps / Unit II: Sales Contract]`
168. **Proximate Cause (Causa Proxima):** Fundamental insurance rule establishing that the insurer is liable only for damage caused by an insured peril that is the direct, proximate, and dominant cause of the loss. `[Unit III: Insurance]`

### Q

169. **Quantitative Restrictions (QRs):** Explicit government limits placed on the absolute physical quantity or monetary volume of specific commodities permitted to enter or leave a nation (prohibited by GATT Art. XI). `[Unit I: WTO]`

### R

170. **RCMC:** Registration-cum-Membership Certificate; a 5-year statutory certificate issued by an authorized Export Promotion Council or Commodity Board certifying an exporter's registration, mandatory for claiming FTP benefits. `[Unit I: Steps / Agencies]`
171. **Recourse Factoring:** A factoring agreement where the Factor advances cash against invoices but retains full legal right to recover advanced funds from the exporter if the foreign buyer fails to pay upon invoice maturity. `[Unit III: Factoring & Forfaiting]`
172. **Red Clause Letter of Credit:** A specialized LC containing a clause authorizing the advising/negotiating bank to advance pre-shipment funds to the exporter before presentation of shipping documents. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
173. **Revocable Letter of Credit:** A Letter of Credit that could historically be cancelled or amended by the issuing bank at any moment without notice to the exporter; obsolete and banned by UCP 600 Art. 3. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
174. **Revolving Letter of Credit:** An LC where the credit limit is automatically replenished upon utilization or maturity without requiring separate amendments, used for continuous recurring shipments. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
175. **Risk Management System (RMS):** An automated statistical risk-profiling computer module operating inside customs ICES that facilitates compliant cargo without physical examination (Green Channel). `[Unit II: EDI]`
176. **RoDTEP:** Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products; Indian government scheme reimbursing un-refunded central, state, and local embedded taxes (VAT on fuel, electricity duty, mandi tax) to exporters. `[Unit II: Regulatory Framework]`
177. **Running Account Facility:** A trade credit arrangement allowing star export houses to draw pre-shipment packing credit advances continuously from banks prior to receiving individual export orders. `[Unit III: Pre-Shipment Finance]`

### S

178. **Safeguard Duty:** An emergency temporary border duty imposed by an importing government under GATT Article XIX to shield domestic industry against an unforeseen, severe surge in imported goods. `[Unit I: WTO]`
179. **Sales Contract:** The primary, legally binding cross-border commercial agreement executed between foreign buyer and seller specifying product details, quantity, Incoterms 2020, price, delivery, and dispute settlement. `[Unit II: Sales Contract]`
180. **SCM Agreement:** Agreement on Subsidies and Countervailing Measures; WTO agreement establishing multilateral discipline over domestic export subsidies and defining remedies for injured importing nations. `[Unit I: WTO]`
181. **SCOMET:** Special Chemicals, Organisms, Materials, Equipment and Technologies; the Indian national export control list regulating dual-use military and civilian items subject to inter-ministerial licensing. `[Unit II: Regulatory Framework]`
182. **Shipping Bill:** The comprehensive statutory customs declaration filed electronically on ICEGATE by an exporter under Section 50 of the Customs Act, 1962, seeking permission to export goods. `[Unit I: Documentation / Unit II: Shipment]`
183. **Sight Letter of Credit:** An LC requiring the issuing or confirming bank to make payment immediately upon presentation of strictly compliant shipping documents. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
184. **Single Undertaking:** The foundational WTO constitutional rule establishing that all multilateral agreements negotiated during a trade round form a single indivisible package that must be accepted entirely. `[Unit I: WTO]`
185. **Single Window Interface for Facilitating Trade (SWIFT):** An Indian customs system on ICEGATE allowing importers and exporters to submit a single integrated declaration that automatically obtains clearance from 6 Partner Government Agencies. `[Unit II: EDI]`
186. **SOFR (Secured Overnight Financing Rate):** The primary benchmark interest rate referenced in international financial markets for floating-rate dollar lending, commonly used to price PCFC loans. `[Unit III: Pre-Shipment Finance]`
187. **SPS Agreement:** Sanitary and Phytosanitary Measures; WTO agreement allowing nations to enforce food safety and animal/plant health quarantine rules based strictly on scientific risk assessment. `[Unit I: WTO]`
188. **Standby Letter of Credit (SBLC):** A bank payment guarantee operating as a financial backstop, drawn upon only if the applicant defaults on underlying performance or payment commitments. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
189. **STC (State Trading Corporation of India):** A premier government-owned enterprise handling bulk imports and exports of agricultural commodities and essential sovereign supplies. `[Unit I: Agencies]`
190. **Strict Compliance (Doctrine of):** Bedrock judicial rule governing Letters of Credit establishing that documents presented by an exporter must mirror the terms and conditions of the credit exactly, with zero material discrepancy. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
191. **Subrogation:** The legal transfer of the insured's rights of recovery against negligent third parties (carriers) to the marine insurance company upon full settlement of an indemnity claim. `[Unit III: Insurance]`

### T

192. **Tariff Binding:** Legal commitment made by a WTO member government in its official tariff schedule specifying the maximum ceiling customs rate applicable to imports from all other members. `[Unit I: WTO]`
193. **TBT Agreement:** Technical Barriers to Trade; WTO agreement ensuring that mandatory technical regulations, product standards, and testing procedures do not create unnecessary trade obstacles. `[Unit I: WTO]`
194. **TFA (Trade Facilitation Agreement):** Landmark multilateral WTO treaty adopted at Bali in 2013 aimed at expediting the movement, release, and customs clearance of cross-border goods. `[Unit I: WTO]`
195. **TPCI:** Trade Promotion Council of India; trade promotional association focused on international business development, consumer goods exports, and organizing the IndusFood international expo. `[Unit I: Agencies]`
196. **Transferable Letter of Credit:** An LC that expressly designates that the original beneficiary (middleman/merchant exporter) may instruct the bank to make the credit available in whole or part to one or more secondary suppliers. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
197. **TRIMS:** Trade-Related Investment Measures; WTO agreement banning domestic investment restrictions that discriminate against foreign products or impose local content mandates. `[Unit I: WTO]`
198. **TRIPS:** Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights; comprehensive WTO agreement establishing binding minimum global standards for patents, copyrights, trademarks, and industrial designs. `[Unit I: WTO]`
199. **Two-Factor System:** An international factoring operational architecture where an Export Factor in the seller's country collaborates with an Import Factor in the buyer's country via the FCI network to manage credit risks and collections. `[Unit III: Factoring & Forfaiting]`

### U

200. **UCP 600:** Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (2007 Revision, ICC Publication No. 600); the universal private international codification governing Letter of Credit transactions globally. `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
201. **UN Layout Key (UNLK):** The United Nations standard (ISO 6422) establishing a master dimensional layout on standard A4 paper for all international trade documents, forming the base of the Aligned Documentation System. `[Unit I: Documentation]`
202. **URC 522:** Uniform Rules for Collections (1995 Revision, ICC Publication No. 522); the standardized global codification regulating bank procedures for documentary collections (D/P and D/A). `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
203. **Usance Letter of Credit (Time LC):** A Letter of Credit where payment is deferred and payable on an agreed future maturity date (e.g., 60, 90, or 120 days from the Bill of Lading date). `[Unit II: Payment Methods]`
204. **Utmost Good Faith (Uberrimae Fidei):** The foundational legal doctrine of marine insurance requiring both the proposer and underwriter to fully disclose all material circumstances affecting the assessed marine risk. `[Unit III: Insurance]`

### W

205. **WCO (World Customs Organization):** Intergovernmental organization based in Brussels representing 180+ customs administrations, responsible for developing and maintaining the international Harmonized System nomenclature. `[Unit III: Harmonized System]`
206. **Without Recourse:** A legal condition in trade finance (mandatory in Forfaiting) meaning that the financing entity assumes 100% of the default risk and has no legal right to demand refund from the exporter if the buyer defaults. `[Unit III: Factoring & Forfaiting]`
207. **WTO (World Trade Organization):** The premier multilateral intergovernmental organization based in Geneva that regulates, negotiates, and enforces rules governing international trade between 164 member nations. `[Unit I: WTO]`

### Y

208. **York-Antwerp Rules:** The internationally recognized codification of standard maritime rules governing the adjustment and mathematical apportionment of General Average sacrifice and expenditures. `[Unit III: Insurance]`

### Z

209. **Zero-Rated Supply:** Tax classification under Section 16 of India's IGST Act, 2017 whereby export of goods and services is taxed at 0%, allowing exporters to export under LUT without tax or claim full refund of accumulated input tax credits. `[Unit II: Central Excise / Regulatory]`
