### 1. GATT 1947 vs WTO 1995

| Parameter | GATT 1947 | WTO 1995 |
|---|---|---|
| **Legal Nature** | Provisional multilateral treaty without an institutional founding charter. | Permanent international intergovernmental organization established by treaty (Marrakesh Agreement). |
| **Institutional Secretariat** | Ad-hoc de facto secretariat; no formal legal personality. | Full international legal personality; formal secretariat based in Geneva, Switzerland. |
| **Scope of Coverage** | Regulated physical merchandise (goods) trade exclusively. | Covers Goods (GATT 1994), Services (GATS), and Intellectual Property (TRIPS). |
| **Plurilateral vs Multilateral** | Allowed "Grandfather rights" and optional plurilateral codes; members could cherry-pick agreements. | Strict **Single Undertaking** principle (*nothing is agreed until everything is agreed*); all agreements mandatory. |
| **Dispute Settlement** | Consensus-based; losing party could veto panel formation and block adoption of adverse reports. | Binding dispute system (DSU); reports adopted automatically under **Negative / Reverse Consensus**. |
| **Membership Status** | Signatories were termed "Contracting Parties". | Sovereign nations and customs territories are official "Members" (164 members). |

### 2. Most-Favoured-Nation (MFN) vs National Treatment (NT)

| Parameter | Most-Favoured-Nation (MFN) | National Treatment (NT) |
|---|---|---|
| **Statutory Article** | **GATT Article I** (and GATS Article II, TRIPS Article 4). | **GATT Article III** (and GATS Article XVII, TRIPS Article 3). |
| **Point of Application** | At the border, upon importation or exportation. | Inside the domestic market, **strictly AFTER goods cross customs**. |
| **Core Target** | Prohibits discrimination between *different foreign trading partners*. | Prohibits discrimination between *imported goods and domestic goods*. |
| **Border Tariffs Permitted?** | Yes, customs tariffs are permitted provided they apply equally to all MFN partners. | Not applicable; deals with internal sales taxes, VAT/GST, and domestic safety regulations. |
| **Core Prohibited Action** | Levying a 10% duty on Chinese steel but 20% on Japanese steel without a valid FTA. | Imposing 5% GST on Indian-made whiskey but 18% GST on imported Scottish whiskey inside India. |
| **Statutory Exceptions** | Regional Free Trade Agreements (GATT Art. XXIV), GSP schemes for developing nations. | Government procurement for state consumption, domestic agricultural subsidies paid to producers. |

### 3. Commercial Invoice vs Proforma Invoice

| Parameter | Proforma Invoice | Commercial Invoice |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature of Document** | Preliminary quotation / offer or invitation to treat. | Binding legal demand for payment and primary accounting document. |
| **Stage of Issuance** | Pre-contractual phase, before production or shipment. | Issued upon or after cargo dispatch / shipment. |
| **Legal Obligation** | Does not create a legal debt; subject to buyer acceptance. | Creates a definitive financial liability on the buyer to pay the specified price. |
| **Role in Banking** | Used by the buyer to apply for an Import License and open a Letter of Credit. | Mandatory core document submitted to banks to claim payment under an LC or collection. |
| **Customs Assessment** | Not accepted for definitive customs duty assessment. | Primary statutory document for customs valuation and duty computation. |
| **Accounting Entry** | Never entered into financial ledgers or accounts books. | Formally entered into sales journals, accounts receivable, and debtor ledgers. |

### 4. Clean Bill of Lading vs Claused / Foul Bill of Lading

| Parameter | Clean Bill of Lading | Claused / Foul / Dirty Bill of Lading |
|---|---|---|
| **Carrier's Notation** | Contains no superimposed clause declaring defective goods or packaging. | Contains explicit written remarks noting damage (e.g., *"3 drums leaking", "cartons wet"*). |
| **Bank Acceptability** | **Universally acceptable** to commercial banks under UCP 600 Article 27. | **Strictly rejected by banks** unless the LC explicitly permits a claused B/L. |
| **Carrier's Liability** | Carrier is presumed to have received goods in apparent good external order. | Carrier protects itself against liability for pre-existing cargo packaging damage. |
| **Title Quality** | First-class negotiable document of title, readily accepted by buyers. | Defective document of title; buyer may refuse cargo and dishonour draft. |
| **Insurance Claim Impact** | Facilitates smooth marine insurance claims for transit damage. | Marine insurers will reject transit damage claims caused by pre-existing defects noted on B/L. |
| **UCP 600 Provision** | Article 27 mandates: *"A bank will only accept a clean transport document."* | Article 27 defines an unclean document as one bearing an express clause declaring defective condition. |

### 5. Ocean Bill of Lading (B/L) vs Air Waybill (AWB)

| Parameter | Ocean Bill of Lading (B/L) | Air Waybill (AWB) |
|---|---|---|
| **Mode of Transport** | Maritime ocean shipping. | Air freight. |
| **Document of Title?** | **YES.** Legally represents ownership of goods; negotiable by endorsement. | **NO.** Strictly non-negotiable; never confers title to goods. |
| **Cargo Delivery Rule** | Carrier delivers cargo only upon surrender of an original endorsed B/L. | Carrier delivers cargo directly to the named consignee upon identification without original AWB. |
| **Governing Law / Rules** | Hague-Visby Rules / Hamburg Rules / Rotterdam Rules. | Warsaw Convention / Montreal Convention. |
| **Number of Originals** | Issued typically in a set of 3 negotiable originals (3/3). | Issued in a set of 3 originals (for carrier, consignee, shipper) plus copies; all non-negotiable. |
| **Consignee Naming** | Commonly drawn *"To Order"* or *"To Order of Shipper"* to preserve title. | Always drawn to a specific named consignee (e.g., *"ABC GmbH"*). |

### 6. Marine Insurance Policy vs Marine Insurance Certificate

| Parameter | Marine Insurance Policy | Marine Insurance Certificate |
|---|---|---|
| **Legal Status** | Complete, standalone legal contract of marine insurance. | Abbreviated declaration certificate issued under an overarching Open Cover policy. |
| **Content Depth** | Contains all statutory terms, warranties, exceptions, and complete Institute Cargo Clauses. | Summarizes shipment specifics (vessel, voyage, insured sum, marks) and references the master policy. |
| **Legal Actionability** | Fully admissible in courts of law as conclusive legal evidence of insurance. | Depends on the master policy; may require production of the master policy in formal litigation. |
| **UCP 600 Banking Rule** | An insurance policy is **always acceptable** in place of an insurance certificate (Art. 28b). | An insurance certificate is **NOT acceptable** if the LC explicitly specifies an *"insurance policy"*. |
| **Issuing Authority** | Underwritten and signed exclusively by the insurance company. | Can be issued by the insurer or authorized insurance broker under open cover. |
| **Operational Use** | Used for single specific shipments or long-term specialized project exports. | Used by high-frequency regular exporters shipping daily/weekly under an Open Cover. |

### 7. Shipping Bill vs Bill of Entry

| Parameter | Shipping Bill | Bill of Entry |
|---|---|---|
| **Transaction Flow** | **Exportation** (goods leaving India). | **Importation** (goods arriving into India). |
| **Statutory Section** | Section 50 of the Customs Act, 1962. | Section 46 of the Customs Act, 1962. |
| **Filing Party** | Exporter or their authorized Customs Broker. | Importer or their authorized Customs Broker. |
| **Statutory Milestone Order** | **Let Export Order (LEO)** under Section 51. | **Out of Charge (OOC)** under Section 47. |
| **Colour Classification (Physical)**| White (Export under Duty Drawback), Pink (Dutiable export), Yellow (Duty-free export). | White (Home consumption), Yellow (Into-Bond/Warehouse), Green (Ex-Bond release). |
| **Primary Regulatory Purpose** | Verification of export restrictions, incentive claims (RoDTEP), and e-BRC tracking. | Assessment and collection of basic customs duty, IGST, and enforcement of import quotas. |

### 8. Documentary Collection: D/P (Sight) vs D/A (Usance)

| Parameter | D/P (Documents Against Payment) | D/A (Documents Against Acceptance) |
|---|---|---|
| **Tenure of Payment** | **At Sight** (cash on demand). | **Usance / Term** (30, 60, 90, or 180 days from B/L date). |
| **Document Release Trigger** | Released only after the buyer makes **immediate, full cash payment**. | Released upon the buyer **signing (accepting) a Bill of Exchange**. |
| **Exporter's Control of Cargo** | Exporter retains control of goods through the bank until paid. | Exporter surrenders control of goods; buyer collects cargo before paying. |
| **Credit Risk to Exporter** | Low credit risk; if buyer refuses payment, goods remain at port. | **High credit risk**; buyer has taken goods and may default on maturity date. |
| **Default Remedy** | Exporter can re-ship goods back, find alternate buyer, or auction cargo. | Exporter holds an accepted bill of exchange and must file cross-border legal suit for debt. |
| **Governing Framework** | ICC Uniform Rules for Collections (URC 522). | ICC Uniform Rules for Collections (URC 522). |

---

### 9. Letter of Credit (LC) vs Documentary Collection

| Parameter | Letter of Credit (LC) | Documentary Collection (D/P & D/A) |
|---|---|---|
| **Bank's Financial Liability** | **Primary payment undertaking**: The issuing bank guarantees payment. | **Zero payment liability**: Banks act purely as collecting conduits. |
| **Governing ICC Rules** | **ICC UCP 600** (Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits). | **ICC URC 522** (Uniform Rules for Collections). |
| **Transaction Cost** | High banking fees (issuance, advising, confirmation, amendment, discrepancy fees). | Low banking commissions; minimal documentary overhead. |
| **Basis of Settlement** | Settlement based on **strict compliance of documents** alone. | Settlement based on buyer's willingness/ability to pay or accept. |
| **Discrepancy Rejection** | Bank strictly rejects payment if documents deviate from credit terms. | Bank simply presents whatever documents the exporter sends; no technical checking. |
| **Exporter Risk Level** | Very low (especially if confirmed by a reputable domestic bank). | Moderate to High (buyer may refuse documents or default on usance maturity). |

### 10. Letter of Credit (LC) vs Open Account

| Parameter | Letter of Credit (LC) | Open Account |
|---|---|---|
| **Security for Exporter** | High security; independent bank payment undertaking. | Zero security; reliant entirely on buyer's commercial solvency and integrity. |
| **Security for Importer** | Moderate; buyer commits credit lines before inspecting physical goods. | Maximum security; buyer receives, inspects, and sells goods before paying. |
| **Cash Flow Impact** | Ties up buyer's bank credit limits and collateral. | Frees buyer working capital; severely strains exporter working capital. |
| **Document Routing** | Documents routed through international banking channels under UCP 600. | Documents mailed directly from exporter to buyer without bank intervention. |
| **Typical Market Context** | High-value capital goods, new buyers, politically volatile nations. | Established long-term partnerships, inter-company multinational transfers. |
| **Financing Mechanism** | Discounted easily via negotiation or pre-shipment packing credit. | Financed via export factoring or post-shipment invoice discounting. |

### 11. Revocable vs Irrevocable Letter of Credit

| Parameter | Revocable Letter of Credit | Irrevocable Letter of Credit |
|---|---|---|
| **Unilateral Modification** | Could be amended or cancelled by issuing bank at any time without notice. | Cannot be amended or cancelled without the formal consent of **ALL** parties. |
| **Commercial Protection** | Offered virtually zero security to the exporter; buyer could cancel mid-transit. | Provides absolute legal and financial payment security to the exporter. |
| **Current Status under UCP 600**| **Obsolete and abolished.** UCP 600 Article 3 deems all credits irrevocable. | Standard universal instrument used in international trade today. |
| **Pre-Shipment Finance** | Banks would not sanction packing credit advances against revocable LCs. | Fully eligible for concessional pre-shipment packing credit advances. |
| **Amendment Protocol** | No amendment protocol required; bank simply altered terms at will. | Amendments require formal acceptance by the beneficiary exporter. |
| **Legal enforceability** | Weak; issuing bank had absolute discretionary cancellation power. | Legally binding independent undertaking enforceable in international arbitration. |

---

### 12. Confirmed vs Unconfirmed Letter of Credit

| Parameter | Confirmed Letter of Credit | Unconfirmed Letter of Credit |
|---|---|---|
| **Number of Bank Undertakings** | **Two independent undertakings** (Issuing Bank + Confirming Bank). | **Single undertaking** (Issuing Bank only). |
| **Sovereign / Political Risk** | **Completely absorbed by Confirming Bank** (located in exporter's country). | Exporter bears foreign country risk (war, revolution, transfer restrictions). |
| **Issuing Bank Insolvency Risk**| If the issuing bank collapses, the **Confirming Bank is still legally bound to pay**. | If the issuing bank collapses, the exporter suffers total financial loss. |
| **Cost / Bank Charges** | Higher cost; confirmation fee charged based on country risk rating. | Lower cost; only standard advising and handling fees apply. |
| **Mandatory Context** | Essential when exporting to politically volatile or foreign-exchange starved nations. | Standard when exporting to OECD economies with AAA-rated global banks. |
| **UCP 600 Provision** | Governed by Article 8 (obligations of Confirming Bank). | Governed by Article 7 (obligations of Issuing Bank). |

### 13. Transferable LC vs Back-to-Back LC

| Parameter | Transferable Letter of Credit | Back-to-Back Letter of Credit |
|---|---|---|
| **Number of LCs Involved** | **Single master credit** transferred in whole or part to second beneficiaries. | **Two separate, independent credits** (Master LC + Secondary LC). |
| **Express Authorization** | Must be explicitly designated as **"Transferable"** in field 40B by issuing bank. | Does not require authorization in master LC; intermediary applies independently. |
| **Intermediary Bank Liability** | Transferring bank acts as an agent; does not pledge its own creditworthiness. | Intermediary's bank issues a separate LC, pledging its own independent credit. |
| **Transfer Restrictions** | Can only be transferred once; second beneficiary cannot transfer to a third. | No transfer restriction; secondary supplier receives an ordinary independent LC. |
| **Substitution of Invoices** | Middleman can substitute their own invoice and draft to capture profit margin. | Intermediary presents secondary supplier's documents under master LC. |
| **UCP 600 Governing Article** | Specifically governed by **Article 38** of UCP 600. | Not specifically defined in UCP 600; treated as two separate standard credits. |

### 14. FOB vs CIF (Incoterms 2020)

| Parameter | FOB (Free On Board) | CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight) |
|---|---|---|
| **Main Carriage Freight** | Contracted and paid by the **BUYER**. | Contracted and paid by the **SELLER** to destination port. |
| **Marine Cargo Insurance** | Arranged and paid by the **BUYER** (for their own account). | Contracted and paid by the **SELLER** in favour of buyer (min. ICC C). |
| **Transfer of Cargo Risk** | When goods pass **on board the vessel at the port of shipment**. | When goods pass **on board the vessel at the port of shipment** (same as FOB!). |
| **Suitable Mode of Transport** | Non-containerized maritime / inland waterway transport only. | Non-containerized maritime / inland waterway transport only. |
| **Price Composition** | Cost of production + domestic transport + dock handling + export clearance. | FOB price + Ocean freight to destination + Marine insurance premium. |
| **Common Misconception** | Students think CIF risk transfers at destination; **it transfers at origin!** | Students correctly note seller pays freight, but fail to realize risk transfers early. |

### 15. CIF vs CIP (Incoterms 2020)

| Parameter | CIF (Cost, Insurance & Freight) | CIP (Carriage & Insurance Paid To) |
|---|---|---|
| **Permitted Transport Mode** | **Sea and inland waterway transport only.** | **Any mode of transport** (Air, road, rail, multimodal, ocean container). |
| **Mandatory Insurance Level** | **ICC (C)** - Basic minimum named perils cover. | **ICC (A) - All Risks** (widest marine coverage). |
| **Delivery / Risk Point** | When goods are loaded **on board the vessel** at loading port. | When goods are handed over to the **first carrier** at origin depot/factory. |
| **Container Appropriateness** | Inappropriate for modern container trade (ICC recommends CIP). | **Highly recommended for all containerized manufactured exports.** |
| **Named Place in Contract** | Must specify the **named destination seaport** (e.g., CIF Hamburg). | Must specify the **named destination place/depot** (e.g., CIP Munich Airport). |
| **Incoterms 2020 Revision** | Retained traditional low-level insurance default (ICC C). | **Deliberately upgraded** insurance requirement from ICC (C) to ICC (A). |

### 16. EXW vs DDP (Incoterms 2020)

| Parameter | EXW (Ex Works) | DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) |
|---|---|---|
| **Seller's Obligation** | **Absolute minimum obligation** across all 11 Incoterms. | **Absolute maximum obligation** across all 11 Incoterms. |
| **Delivery Location** | At seller's factory/warehouse gate in exporting country. | At buyer's premises/warehouse in destination country. |
| **Loading on Vehicle** | Buyer is responsible for loading goods onto vehicle at seller's door. | Seller arranges and pays for all transit, offloading, and delivery. |
| **Export Customs Clearance** | Buyer must arrange export clearance (often impossible for foreign buyers). | Seller handles and pays for all export customs clearance formalities. |
| **Import Customs Clearance** | Buyer handles import clearance and pays all foreign duties/taxes. | **Seller handles foreign import customs and pays import duties and local VAT.** |
| **Risk Spectrum** | Risk transfers immediately at seller's factory before loading. | Risk transfers only upon physical arrival at buyer's warehouse. |

---

### 17. Container Freight Station (CFS) vs Inland Container Depot (ICD)

| Parameter | Container Freight Station (CFS) | Inland Container Depot (ICD) |
|---|---|---|
| **Geographical Location** | Port-adjacent; located in close vicinity to sea or air customs terminals. | Located deep in the inland hinterland, away from maritime coastal ports. |
| **Customs Independence** | Acts as an operational extension of the main maritime customs house. | **Independent customs station** with its own port code, commissioners, and staff. |
| **Rail Transport Link** | Usually linked to port by road trailers for fast shuttle transfers. | Connected to sea ports via dedicated railway container rail lines (CONCOR). |
| **Primary Core Function** | Stuffing and destuffing of LCL cargo; temporary container holding. | Full multimodal consolidation, customs clearance, bonded warehousing, and rail staging. |
| **Shipping Bill Clearance** | Exporter files Shipping Bill; final LEO often granted at port gate. | Exporter files Shipping Bill and obtains **full LEO inland at the ICD**. |
| **Bill of Lading Issuance** | B/L issued after container reaches port dock and is loaded on board. | Carrier can issue a **Combined Transport B/L** directly from the ICD. |

---

### 18. FCL (Full Container Load) vs LCL (Less than Container Load)

| Parameter | FCL (Full Container Load) | LCL (Less than Container Load) |
|---|---|---|
| **Cargo Volume** | Cargo volume is sufficient to fill a full 20ft or 40ft container. | Volume is small; cannot economically justify a dedicated container. |
| **Number of Shippers** | Single exporter / shipper per container. | Multiple unrelated exporters consolidate cargo into a single shared box. |
| **Stuffing / Packing Point** | Stuffed and customs-sealed directly at the exporter's factory. | Stuffed and consolidated by a freight forwarder/custodian at a CFS. |
| **Risk of Handling Damage** | Very low; container is sealed at factory and opened only by foreign buyer. | Higher risk; cargo handled multiple times during consolidation and destuffing. |
| **Freight Cost Structure** | Flat rate per container (box rate) regardless of weight/volume up to max. | Charged on weight or measurement (W/M) basis per revenue ton / CBM. |
| **Transport Document** | Ocean Master Bill of Lading (MBL) issued directly by shipping line. | Forwarder issues a House Bill of Lading (HBL) to each individual shipper. |

### 19. Demurrage vs Detention

| Parameter | Demurrage | Detention |
|---|---|---|
| **Fee Recipient / Beneficiary** | Paid to the **Port Authority / Terminal Custodian** (or shipping line for port space). | Paid to the **Ocean Container Shipping Line**. |
| **Nature of Equipment/Property**| Storage fee for **occupying port/terminal land and ground storage space**. | Equipment rental penalty for **holding the shipping line's container**. |
| **Location of Occurrence** | Inside the customs-bonded port / terminal premises. | Outside the port terminal (e.g., at importer's factory or transport depot). |
| **Free Period Allowance** | Short; typically 3 to 5 calendar days from vessel discharge. | Moderate; typically 7 to 14 days from container gate-out to empty gate-in. |
| **Export Context** | Consignment gated into port but held up due to customs query beyond free days. | Exporter picks up empty box from depot but delays returning stuffed box to port. |
| **Import Context** | Importer delays filing Bill of Entry; cargo sits on wharf accumulating rent. | Importer moves container to factory to destuff but delays returning empty box to depot. |

### 20. Traditional Central Excise vs Modern GST Export Clearance

| Parameter | Traditional Central Excise Regime | Modern Post-GST Regime |
|---|---|---|
| **Governing Statute** | Central Excise Act, 1944 and Central Excise Rules, 2002. | Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST) Act, 2017 (Section 16). |
| **Export Supply Classification** | Exempted or subject to rebate of duty paid under Rule 18. | Explicitly classified as a **"Zero-Rated Supply"**. |
| **Duty-Free Export Instrument** | General Bond Form B-1 / Letter of Undertaking under Rule 19. | **Letter of Undertaking (LUT)** filed online annually on the GST portal. |
| **Statutory Removal Document** | Triplicate / Quintuplicate copies of physical form **ARE-1**. | Standard GST Tax Invoice / Commercial Invoice under Rule 46 of CGST Rules. |
| **Duty-Free Input Procurement** | Form **CT-1** issued by central excise superintendent. | Sourcing raw materials under Advance Authorization or claiming refund of unutilized ITC. |
| **Refund Processing Mechanism** | Manual paper claims filed before Maritime Excise Commissioner. | **Fully automated electronic refund** via ICEGATE based on Shipping Bill and GSTR-1 matching. |

### 21. Pre-Shipment Finance vs Post-Shipment Finance

| Parameter | Pre-Shipment Finance (Packing Credit) | Post-Shipment Finance |
|---|---|---|
| **Operational Stage** | Before shipment (procurement, processing, packaging stage). | After shipment (after goods are loaded and B/L is issued). |
| **Primary Financial Purpose** | Working capital to purchase raw materials, manufacture, and pack goods. | Working capital bridging the gap while awaiting overseas buyer payment. |
| **Documentary Requirement** | Confirmed export sales order or Irrevocable Letter of Credit. | Full suite of commercial shipping documents (Invoice, B/L, Insurance, COO). |
| **Method of Disbursement** | Cash credit or loan account; disbursed in stages against supplier bills. | Purchase, discounting, or negotiation of export commercial bills/drafts. |
| **Method of Liquidation** | **Liquidated strictly out of post-shipment export bill proceeds.** | Liquidated out of incoming foreign exchange remittances from overseas buyer. |
| **Maximum Standard Tenure** | Up to 180 days (extendable to 270/360 days in approved cases). | Matched to usance credit period (typically up to 90/180 days). |

### 22. Rupee Packing Credit vs PCFC (Foreign Currency Packing Credit)

| Parameter | Rupee Packing Credit | PCFC (Packing Credit in Foreign Currency) |
|---|---|---|
| **Currency of Disbursement** | Indian Rupees (INR). | Major convertible foreign currency (USD, EUR, GBP, JPY). |
| **Interest Rate Benchmark** | Linked to domestic bank lending rates (Repo / MCLR) minus subvention. | Linked to international benchmark rates (SOFR / EURIBOR) + small spread. |
| **Foreign Exchange Risk** | High; exporter bears currency fluctuation risk unless hedged separately. | **Zero exchange risk (Natural Hedge)**; loan repaid directly from export proceeds. |
| **Eligible Borrowers** | All registered Indian manufacturer and merchant exporters. | Exporters possessing confirmed export orders/LCs denominated in foreign currency. |
| **Conversion Costs** | Exporter incurs rupee-to-foreign currency conversion spreads twice. | Eliminates conversion spreads; funds credited directly to supplier or EEFC account. |
| **IES Interest Subvention** | Covered under government Interest Equalisation Scheme. | Governed by competitive international foreign currency lending spreads. |

### 23. Factoring vs Forfaiting

| Parameter | Export Factoring | Forfaiting |
|---|---|---|
| **Tenure of Credit** | **Short-term** (typically 30 to 180 days). | **Medium to Long-term** (1 to 7 years). |
| **Eligible Goods** | Consumer goods, fast-moving commodities, raw materials. | Capital goods, heavy machinery, large infrastructure project exports. |
| **Commercial Instrument** | Book debts, open account commercial invoices. | **Negotiable instruments** (Bills of Exchange or Promissory Notes). |
| **Bank Guarantee (Aval)** | Generally not required; factor relies on buyer credit evaluation. | **Mandatory international bank Aval** (guarantee) on the bill. |
| **Recourse Status** | Can be **With Recourse** or **Without Recourse**. | **Strictly and 100% WITHOUT RECOURSE**. |
| **Advance Quantum** | 70% to 85% cash advance; remaining 15-30% paid upon debt collection. | **100% of the discounted instrument value** paid immediately. |

---

### 24. Recourse Factoring vs Non-Recourse Factoring

| Parameter | Recourse Factoring | Non-Recourse Factoring |
|---|---|---|
| **Buyer Default Risk** | **Borne by the exporter.** Factor does not insure against credit loss. | **Borne 100% by the Factor.** Factor assumes complete credit risk. |
| **Factor's Recovery Right** | Factor can demand immediate repayment of advanced cash from exporter. | Factor has zero legal right to demand refund from exporter if buyer defaults. |
| **Cost / Commission Fee** | Lower factoring service fee (only finance and ledger administration). | Higher factoring fee (includes credit protection insurance premium). |
| **Balance Sheet Impact** | Exporter must record advance as a contingent financial liability. | **Off-balance sheet financing**; receivables removed entirely from balance sheet. |
| **Trade Dispute Impact** | Exporter handles dispute and repays factor immediately. | If buyer refuses to pay due to a genuine commercial dispute, factor retains recourse. |
| **Popularity in India** | Widely used for domestic trade and established creditworthy buyers. | Standard for cross-border export factoring through the two-factor FCI system. |

### 25. Commercial Risk vs Political Risk in International Trade

| Parameter | Commercial Risk | Political Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Source of Risk** | The private overseas buyer / corporate enterprise. | Sovereign foreign governments, state authorities, or civil upheavals. |
| **Typical Triggers** | Buyer insolvency, bankruptcy, protracted default, repudiation of contract. | Outbreak of war, revolution, civil strife, foreign exchange transfer embargoes. |
| **Exporter Control** | Partially controllable via thorough credit appraisal (Dun & Bradstreet). | Completely uncontrollable by the exporter or buyer. |
| **Primary Mitigation Tool**| Non-recourse factoring, Irrevocable Letter of Credit, private credit insurance. | Irrevocable Confirmed LC, ECGC Political Risk Policy, Bilateral Investment Treaties. |
| **ECGC Cover Percentage** | Covers up to 90% under standard Shipments Comprehensive Risks policy. | Covers up to 90% under standard Shipments Comprehensive Risks policy. |
| **Dispute Exclusion** | ECGC rejects claims arising from commercial quality disputes. | ECGC covers losses arising from unexpected cancellation of import licenses abroad. |

### 26. General Average vs Particular Average

| Parameter | General Average | Particular Average |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature of Loss** | **Intentional, voluntary sacrifice or extraordinary expenditure.** | **Accidental, fortuitous loss or damage** caused by an insured casualty. |
| **Purpose of Sacrifice** | Made reasonably for the **common preservation of ship, cargo, and crew**. | Accidental damage affecting solely one specific owner's cargo. |
| **Apportionment of Loss** | **Shared proportionately** by shipowner, all cargo owners, and freight earner. | **Borne exclusively** by the owner of the damaged cargo or their underwriter. |
| **Governing International Code**| **York-Antwerp Rules** (administered by General Average Adjusters). | Marine Insurance Act, 1963 and Institute Cargo Clauses (ICC A/B/C). |
| **Classic Practical Example** | Jettisoning 100 containers to refloat a vessel grounded on a sandbar. | 50 bags of sugar spoiled because sea water entered a damaged container. |
| **Cargo Release Condition** | Cargo released only after cargo owner signs General Average Bond. | Cargo released immediately through ordinary customs channels upon clearance. |

| Parameter | ICC (C) | ICC (B) | ICC (A) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope of Coverage** | **Minimum Named Perils** (catastrophic maritime accidents only). | **Intermediate Named Perils** (catastrophic + specified natural perils). | **"All Risks"** (covers all transit risks except express exclusions). |
| **Fire & Explosion** | Covered | Covered | Covered |
| **Sinking, Stranding, Capsizing**| Covered | Covered | Covered |
| **Jettison & General Average** | Covered | Covered | Covered |
| **Earthquake, Volcanic Eruption**| Not Covered | Covered | Covered |
| **Washing Overboard & Water Entry**| Not Covered | Covered | Covered |
| **Theft, Pilferage, Hijacking** | Not Covered | Not Covered | Covered |
| **Standard Universal Exclusions**| Inherent Vice, ordinary leakage, improper packaging, delay, unseaworthiness. | Inherent Vice, ordinary leakage, improper packaging, delay, unseaworthiness. | Inherent Vice, ordinary leakage, improper packaging, delay, unseaworthiness. |

### 28. Consignment-Wise Inspection vs In-Process vs Self-Certification

| Parameter | Consignment-Wise Inspection | In-Process Quality Control (IPQC) | Self-Certification Scheme |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Statutory Basis** | Export (Quality Control & Inspection) Act, 1963. | Export (Quality Control & Inspection) Act, 1963. | Export (Quality Control & Inspection) Act, 1963. |
| **Inspection Methodology** | Physical sampling and testing of each individual packed consignment at dock. | Continuous monitoring of raw materials, stages, and plant processes. | Exporter self-audits and certifies compliance on their own authority. |
| **Eligible Exporters** | New exporters, small manufacturers, non-star export houses. | Manufacturing units equipped with approved in-house testing labs. | Star Export Houses, ISO 9001 units with proven zero-complaint record. |
| **Speed of Clearance** | Slowest; goods held at port pending lab testing and certificate issuance. | Moderate; batches released based on production quality audit logs. | **Fastest; immediate customs clearance with zero pre-shipment delays.** |
| **Inspection Agency** | Export Inspection Agency (EIA) officers physically visit and draw samples. | EIA conducts periodic surprise process audits at the factory. | No routine physical inspection; subject to random post-clearance audit. |

### 29. Duty Drawback: Section 74 vs Section 75 Customs Act

| Parameter | Section 74 (Drawback on Re-Export) | Section 75 (Drawback on Export Manufacturing) |
|---|---|---|
| **Transaction Nature** | Re-export of imported goods in the same state or after limited domestic use. | Export of finished manufactured goods incorporating imported raw materials. |
| **Identification Requirement** | Mandatory physical proof that the exported goods are the exact goods imported. | Relies on standard input-output norms (SION) or average industry utilization. |
| **Drawback Quantum** | **Up to 98% of customs import duty** paid (reduced if goods were used). | Granted according to fixed **All Industry Rates (AIR)** or Brand Rates. |
| **Time Limit for Re-Export** | Goods must be exported within **2 years** from the date of duty payment. | Based on manufacturing export within standard period of shipment. |
| **Application Procedure** | Claim filed on Shipping Bill with original import Bill of Entry and duty challan. | Automatically processed electronically on ICEGATE via Shipping Bill details. |
| **Disbursement Authority** | Customs house where import took place. | Credited directly to exporter's bank account via electronic customs scroll. |

### 30. WCO 6-Digit HS Code vs Indian 8-Digit ITC(HS) Code

| Parameter | WCO 6-Digit HS Code | Indian 8-Digit ITC(HS) Code |
|---|---|---|
| **Administering Body** | World Customs Organization (WCO, Brussels). | Directorate General of Foreign Trade & CBIC (Ministry of Finance). |
| **Geographical Harmonization** | **Universally identical** across 200+ countries covering 98% of global trade. | Unique to the Republic of India; digits 7 and 8 define national tariff items. |
| **Structural Breakdown** | Chapter (Digits 1–2), Heading (Digits 3–4), Subheading (Digits 5–6). | WCO Subheading (Digits 1–6) + National Tariff Sub-Classification (Digits 7–8). |
| **Amendment Frequency** | Major amendments promulgated every 5 years (latest: HS 2022, 7th Edition). | Updated continuously via annual Union Budgets and DGFT Trade Notifications. |
| **Level of Granularity** | Identifies the broad commodity type (e.g., *0902.10 = Green tea <= 3kg*). | Identifies precise trade specification (e.g., *0902.10.10 = Content <= 25g*). |
| **Legal Status in India** | Foundation of the First Schedule to the Customs Tariff Act, 1975. | Mandatory statutory code required on every Indian Shipping Bill and Bill of Entry. |
