# Unit II: International Trade Operations
## Mnemonics, Exam Traps & Rapid-Fire Facts

### 1. High-Yield Memory Aids & Mnemonics

#### A. The Incoterms 2020 Groups: "E - F - C - D" ("Exit, Freight-Free, Carriage-Paid, Delivered")
- **Group E (Departure):** **EXW** (Ex Works) — Buyer takes everything from seller's factory gate. Minimum seller liability.
- **Group F (Main Carriage Unpaid by Seller):** **FCA, FAS, FOB** — Seller delivers to carrier/port named by buyer; buyer pays main ocean/air freight.
- **Group C (Main Carriage Paid by Seller):** **CPT, CIP, CFR, CIF** — Seller contracts and pays freight to destination; **CRITICAL: Risk transfers to buyer at origin!**
- **Group D (Arrival / Destination):** **DAP, DPU, DDP** — Seller bears all costs and risks to destination country. Maximum seller liability.

#### B. The 4 Sea/Inland Waterway-Only Terms: "FAS - FOB - CFR - CIF"
- *Mnemonic:* **"Fish And Ships For Cargo In Flotilla"**
- All 4 terms apply **only when cargo is loaded onto a vessel**:
  - FAS = Alongside Ship (at quay/barge)
  - FOB = On board the vessel
  - CFR = On board + Freight paid to destination
  - CIF = On board + Freight + Marine Insurance paid to destination
- *Golden Rule:* If cargo is containerized or moved by multimodal transport (road/rail/air), ICC warns you **MUST NOT use FOB/CIF**; use **FCA/CIP** instead!

#### C. The International Payment Risk Ladder: "A - L - D - O" ("Always Look Down On risk")
From safest to riskiest for the exporter:
- **A** — **Advance Payment** (100% upfront cash)
- **L** — **Letter of Credit** (Bank's independent guarantee under UCP 600)
- **D** — **Documentary Collection** (D/P Sight then D/A Usance under URC 522)
- **O** — **Open Account** (Ship first, pay 60–90 days later; maximum default exposure)

#### D. Bill of Entry Color Coding: "W - Y - G" ("White House, Yellow Warehouse, Green Go")
- **White Bill of Entry:** For direct **Home Consumption** (duty paid immediately upon arrival).
- **Yellow / Buff Bill of Entry:** For **Warehousing (Into-Bond)** under Section 59 (duty deferred; stored in customs bonded warehouse).
- **Green Bill of Entry:** For **Ex-Bond Clearance** under Section 68 (removing stored goods from customs bonded warehouse for home consumption upon paying duty).

#### E. Sales Contract 10 Essential Clauses: "P-D-Q-P-D | P-I-F-G-A"
- **P**reamble (Parties & Corporate Details)
- **D**escription of Goods & ITC(HS)
- **Q**uantity & Tolerance (+/- 5%)
- **P**rice & Currency with Incoterms 2020
- **D**elivery Schedule & Transshipment
- **P**ayment Method (LC / DP / DA)
- **I**nspection Agency (SGS / Pre-shipment)
- **F**orce Majeure (Act of God / War)
- **G**overning Law (Choice of Law)
- **A**rbitration & Jurisdiction (SIAC / ICC / ICA)

### 2. Top 20 Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

| # | What Students Often Think (The Trap) | What is Actually True (The Reality) | Why Examiners Test It |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | In CIF contracts, the seller bears the risk of loss while goods cross the ocean. | In CIF, the seller pays freight and insurance, but **the risk of loss transfers to the buyer the moment goods are loaded on board at the origin port**. If the ship sinks, the buyer claims from the insurance policy provided by the seller. | Most frequently tested Incoterms concept. |
| 2 | FOB can be used for air freight shipments from New Delhi airport. | FOB is strictly a **maritime-only term**. For air cargo or containerized transport, the correct Incoterm 2020 rule is **FCA (Free Carrier)**. | Multi-modal vs sea-only rule test. |
| 3 | In Incoterms 2020, CIP and CIF require the exact same insurance cover. | CIF requires minimum cover (**ICC C**). CIP requires maximum all-risks cover (**ICC A**). This was a major divergence introduced in Incoterms 2020. | Tests knowledge of 2020 updates. |
| 4 | Under DPU (Delivered at Place Unloaded), the buyer must arrange the crane to unload the truck/ship. | DPU is the **ONLY Incoterm rule where the seller is legally responsible for unloading the cargo** at destination. Under DAP and DDP, the buyer unloads. | Specific rule mechanic trap. |
| 5 | DDP means the seller delivers goods to the foreign port dock only. | DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) means the seller delivers goods **to the buyer's warehouse door with all import customs duties, clearance fees, and VAT/GST fully paid by the seller**. | Maximum seller liability trap. |
| 6 | An issuing bank can refuse to pay an LC if the buyer complains that the delivered goods are damaged or poor quality. | Under **UCP 600 Article 4 (Autonomy of Credit)**, an LC is completely independent of the underlying goods. If the documents strictly comply on paper, **the bank MUST pay**, even if the buyer claims the goods are defective. | Fundamental bedrock of trade banking. |
| 7 | Under UCP 600, banks have 14 days to examine documents. | Banks have a strict maximum limit of **5 banking days** following the day of presentation to examine documents and issue a notice of refusal (Article 14b). | Exact statutory number. |
| 8 | In Documentary Collection (D/P), the buyer's bank guarantees payment if the buyer defaults. | In Documentary Collections under URC 522, banks act strictly as **remitting/collecting agents**. They do **NOT guarantee payment**. If the buyer refuses to accept the documents, the bank assumes zero financial liability. | LC vs Collection distinction. |
| 9 | D/A collection is safer for the exporter than D/P collection. | D/A is **far riskier** than D/P. Under D/A, the buyer gets the original Bill of Lading and takes the cargo immediately just by signing a future acceptance (promise to pay). If they default at maturity, the exporter has lost both goods and money. | Credit risk sequence trap. |
| 10 | If a sales contract mentions no governing law, Indian law automatically applies everywhere. | In the absence of an explicit governing law clause, cross-border courts determine jurisdiction through complex private international law rules or CISG default rules, often leading to costly foreign litigation. | Contract clause drafting trap. |
| 11 | A Shipping Bill is filed by an importer to bring goods into India. | A **Shipping Bill is filed by an EXPORTER** to clear export goods out of India. An **importer files a Bill of Entry**. | Absolute fundamental document confusion. |
| 12 | Let Export Order (LEO) is issued by the shipping line captain. | LEO is issued by the **Customs Officer (Superintendent / Preventive Officer)** under Section 51 of the Customs Act, 1962, certifying customs clearance. | Authority jurisdiction trap. |
| 13 | Out of Charge (OOC) is granted for export cargo. | OOC is granted strictly for **IMPORT cargo** under Section 47 of the Customs Act, certifying that import duty has been paid and goods are released. | Export vs Import milestone confusion. |
| 14 | Demurrage and Detention are identical terms for the same port fee. | **Demurrage** is paid to the port/terminal custodian for occupying ground space beyond free days. **Detention** is paid to the shipping line for keeping their container outside the port beyond free days. | Practical logistics operational trap. |
| 15 | Central Excise duty must still be paid by all Indian garment and engineering exporters in 2026. | Central Excise was **subsumed into GST on 1 July 2017**. Exporters now operate under **Section 16 of the IGST Act** (LUT or IGST refund). Excise only remains for 5 petroleum products and liquor. | Historical vs present tax law. |
| 16 | Exporting under LUT requires paying 18% IGST upfront and waiting 6 months for a cash refund. | Exporting under LUT (Letter of Undertaking) allows exports with **ZERO upfront tax payment**. No IGST is paid on the export invoice. | GST operational mechanics. |
| 17 | ICEGATE and ICES are two completely different competing government software portals. | **ICES is the internal customs processing software** run by officers. **ICEGATE is the external internet portal** through which trade entities submit documents to ICES. | Customs EDI architecture. |
| 18 | The RMS (Risk Management System) examines 100% of export and import containers physically. | The RMS does the exact opposite: it **facilitates compliant cargo without physical examination** (Green Channel), selecting only high-risk cargo (< 20%) for physical examination. | Automation purpose trap. |
| 19 | SWIFT in international trade customs stands for the bank wire transfer system. | In Indian customs clearance, **SWIFT stands for Single Window Interface for Facilitating Trade**, an electronic platform integrating 6 Partner Government Agencies (PGAs) on ICEGATE. | Acronym collision trap. |
| 20 | SCOMET items can be freely exported without any special license if the value is under USD 10,000. | SCOMET (Dual-Use items) exports **strictly require an explicit authorization from the DGFT inter-ministerial committee**, regardless of shipment value. Unauthorized export is a severe criminal offense under the WMD Act. | High-compliance regulatory trap. |

### 3. Top 50 Rapid-Fire Facts for Instant Recall

1. **FT(D&R) Act Enactment Year:** 1992.
2. **Current Foreign Trade Policy:** FTP 2023 (effective 1 April 2023).
3. **Does FTP 2023 have a 5-year sunset clause?** NO; it is dynamic and open-ended.
4. **Number of pillars in FTP 2023:** 4 pillars.
5. **Entity that issues SCOMET export licenses:** DGFT (Inter-Ministerial Working Group).
6. **Statute governing Indian import/export customs duties:** Customs Act, 1962.
7. **Statute governing cross-border foreign exchange realization:** FEMA, 1999.
8. **Section of Customs Act for filing Shipping Bill:** Section 50.
9. **Section of Customs Act for granting Let Export Order (LEO):** Section 51.
10. **Section of Customs Act for filing Bill of Entry:** Section 46.
11. **Section of Customs Act for granting Out of Charge (OOC):** Section 47.
12. **Section of Customs Act for Import General Manifest (IGM):** Section 30.
13. **IGST Act Section defining Exports as Zero-Rated:** Section 16 of IGST Act, 2017.
14. **Document executed on GST portal for tax-free exports:** Letter of Undertaking (LUT).
15. **Validity of LUT under GST:** One full financial year (1 April to 31 March).
16. **UN Convention governing international commercial sales contracts:** CISG (Vienna Convention 1980).
17. **Standard tolerance clause for bulk cargo:** More or Less clause (+/- 5% or +/- 10%).
18. **International body that publishes Incoterms:** International Chamber of Commerce (ICC, Paris).
19. **Current edition of Incoterms in force:** Incoterms 2020 (effective 1 January 2020).
20. **Total number of Incoterms 2020 rules:** 11 rules.
21. **Number of multimodal (any mode) Incoterms:** 7 rules (EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP).
22. **Number of sea/inland waterway-only Incoterms:** 4 rules (FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF).
23. **The only Incoterm where seller must unload cargo at destination:** DPU (Delivered at Place Unloaded).
24. **Incoterm rule that replaced DAT:** DPU.
25. **Default marine insurance cover required under CIF:** ICC (C) - Minimum cover.
26. **Default marine insurance cover required under CIP:** ICC (A) - All Risks cover.
27. **Incoterm with maximum obligation for the seller:** DDP.
28. **Incoterm with minimum obligation for the seller:** EXW.
29. **Safest payment method for an exporter:** 100% Cash in Advance.
30. **Safest payment method for an importer:** Open Account.
31. **ICC Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits:** UCP 600 (effective July 2007).
32. **ICC Uniform Rules for Collections:** URC 522.
33. **UCP 600 maximum banking days for document examination:** 5 banking days (Article 14b).
34. **Standard document presentation deadline under UCP 600 if unspecified:** Within 21 calendar days from B/L date (Article 14c).
35. **SWIFT message type for issuing an irrevocable LC:** MT 700.
36. **Under UCP 600, are all LCs revocable or irrevocable by default?** Irrevocable (Article 3).
37. **LC type where original beneficiary transfers credit to secondary supplier:** Transferable LC.
38. **LC type with a revolving credit replenishment mechanism:** Revolving LC.
39. **LC clause permitting advance against raw materials before shipping documents:** Red Clause LC.
40. **Documentary collection where buyer pays cash before receiving documents:** D/P (Documents Against Payment).
41. **Documentary collection where buyer accepts a term bill before receiving documents:** D/A (Documents Against Acceptance).
42. **Traditional excise form for export removal under rebate:** Form ARE-1.
43. **Traditional procurement form for duty-free inputs under excise:** Form CT-1.
44. **White Bill of Entry indicates:** Home Consumption.
45. **Yellow Bill of Entry indicates:** Warehousing (Into-Bond).
46. **Green Bill of Entry indicates:** Ex-Bond clearance.
47. **Full form of ICES:** Indian Customs EDI System.
48. **Full form of ICEGATE:** Indian Customs Electronic Commerce/Data Interchange Gateway.
49. **Full form of RMS in customs clearance:** Risk Management System.
50. **Full form of SWIFT in Indian customs clearance:** Single Window Interface for Facilitating Trade.
