# Unit III: Export Finance, Risk Management & Technical Procedures
## Mnemonics, Exam Traps & Rapid-Fire Facts

---

### 1. High-Yield Memory Aids & Mnemonics

#### A. Packing Credit Operational Lifecycle: "O - D - M - L" ("Only Deliver Manufactured Lots")
- **O** — **Order / LC Verification:** Exporter must possess a confirmed export order or Irrevocable LC with a valid IEC.
- **D** — **Disbursement in Stages:** Funds disbursed phased against manufacturing milestones; paid directly to raw material suppliers.
- **M** — **Margin Maintenance:** Bank maintains 10% to 25% margin on FOB value (or production cost).
- **L** — **Liquidation Strictly via Export Bills:** Loan MUST be liquidated by negotiating export shipping bills; never from local cash deposits.

#### B. The 4 Core Functions of Export Factoring: "F - I - L - C" ("Factors Insure Ledger Collections")
- **F** — **Finance:** Immediate cash advance of 70% to 85% against open account / D/A commercial invoices.
- **I** — **Insurance / Credit Protection:** 100% credit default protection in non-recourse factoring.
- **L** — **Ledger Administration:** Maintenance of sales accounting, invoices, and overseas buyer debtor ledgers.
- **C** — **Collection Services:** Professional debt collection handled on the ground by the Import Factor in the buyer's home country.

#### C. Factoring vs Forfaiting: "F-S-I" vs "F-L-B"
- **Factoring = F - S - I:**
  - **F**actoring is **S**hort-term (30–180 days) on **I**nvoices / book debts (recourse or non-recourse).
- **Forfaiting = F - L - B:**
  - **F**orfaiting is **L**ong/Medium-term (1 to 7 years) on **B**ills of Exchange / Promissory Notes with mandatory bank Aval (strictly 100% without recourse).

#### D. The 5 Core Principles of Marine Cargo Insurance: "I - U - I - S - P" ("Inspect Underwriters In Severe Peril")
- **I** — **Insurable Interest:** Exporter/buyer must have a direct financial stake in the safe arrival of cargo at the time of loss.
- **U** — **Utmost Good Faith (Uberrimae Fidei):** Strict duty to disclose all material facts affecting marine risk.
- **I** — **Indemnity:** Policy compensates exact financial loss; insured cannot make a profit from insurance.
- **S** — **Subrogation:** Upon claim settlement, the insurer takes over the insured's rights to sue the negligent shipping carrier.
- **P** — **Proximate Cause (Causa Proxima):** Loss must be directly caused by an insured peril, not by excluded causes (e.g., inherent vice).

#### E. General Rules of Interpretation (GRI Rules 1–6): "T - I - S - A - C - S" ("Top Interpreters Specify All Complex Subheadings")
- **Rule 1: T** — **Terms of Headings & Section/Chapter Notes:** Controls > 90% of all classifications.
- **Rule 2: I** — **Incomplete / Unassembled Articles:** Unfinished goods having essential character classified as complete.
- **Rule 3: S** — **Specific Description over General:** 3(a) Specific; 3(b) Essential character; 3(c) Last numerical heading.
- **Rule 4: A** — **Akin Rule:** Goods classified under heading for goods to which they are most akin.
- **Rule 5: C** — **Cases & Containers:** 5(a) Shaped fitted cases (e.g., violin case); 5(b) Repetitive durable packaging.
- **Rule 6: S** — **Subheadings:** Compare subheadings strictly at the same digit level (single dash vs double dash).

### 2. Top 20 Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

| # | What Students Often Think (The Trap) | What is Actually True (The Reality) | Why Examiners Test It |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Packing Credit can be repaid by depositing cash or using domestic sales revenue. | Packing credit **CANNOT be liquidated from domestic sales**. Under RBI regulations, it must be liquidated strictly out of the proceeds of export bills discounted, purchased, or negotiated. | Fundamental compliance rule of RBI export credit. |
| 2 | PCFC (Packing Credit in Foreign Currency) increases foreign exchange risk for the exporter. | PCFC creates a **Natural Hedge that ELIMINATES foreign exchange risk**. The loan is borrowed in USD/EUR and repaid directly from the incoming USD/EUR export proceeds, avoiding currency conversion fees. | Export finance risk nuance. |
| 3 | Forfaiting can be done on ordinary commercial invoices on open account. | Forfaiting **strictly requires negotiable debt instruments** (Bills of Exchange or Promissory Notes) guaranteed by a reputable international bank via an **Aval** or separate guarantee. | Factoring vs Forfaiting operational trap. |
| 4 | In Forfaiting, if the foreign buyer defaults, the forfaiter can demand money back from the exporter. | Forfaiting is **STRICTLY WITHOUT RECOURSE**. The forfaiter assumes 100% of the commercial and political default risk. They have zero legal right to demand repayment from the exporter. | Core definition of forfaiting. |
| 5 | An Advising Bank in an LC transaction guarantees payment to the exporter. | An Advising Bank **assumes zero financial liability**. It only authenticates the genuineness of the LC. Only a **Confirming Bank** guarantees payment by adding its independent undertaking. | Bank role distinction trap. |
| 6 | In Marine Cargo Insurance, ICC (A) covers every imaginable loss without exception. | ICC (A) is "All Risks", but **strictly excludes statutory general exclusions**: Inherent Vice, ordinary leakage/loss in weight, insufficient/defective packing, wilful misconduct, and unseaworthiness of vessel. | Scope of ICC cover trap. |
| 7 | General Average means the loss is borne solely by the owner of the damaged cargo. | That is **Particular Average**. In **General Average**, cargo is voluntarily sacrificed for the common safety of the ship and all cargo (e.g., jettison), and the financial loss is **shared proportionately among the shipowner, all cargo owners, and freight earner**. | General vs Particular Average confusion. |
| 8 | An exporter can insure cargo for 200% of its CIF value to make a large profit if the ship sinks. | Under the **Principle of Indemnity**, insurance only compensates actual financial loss. Marine cargo is traditionally insured for **CIF + 10%** (to cover the exporter's anticipated profit and incidental handling costs). | Fundamental insurance law principle. |
| 9 | In marine insurance, "Inherent Vice" means the ship captain committed a criminal act. | Inherent Vice refers to the **natural deterioration, rotting, spoilage, or chemical breakdown** of the goods due to their own inherent nature (e.g., fruit rotting, spontaneous combustion of coal). It is universally excluded. | Specialized terminology trap. |
| 10 | ECGC compensates exporters when the overseas buyer rejects goods due to quality disputes. | ECGC **strictly excludes buyer's rejection of goods due to quality disputes or contract breach by the exporter**. ECGC covers insolvency and protracted default, not commercial quality fights. | ECGC policy scope trap. |
| 11 | Export Inspection Council (EIC) conducts quality testing for software exports. | EIC inspects **physical merchandise commodities notified under the Export (QC&I) Act, 1963** (marine products, food grains, chemicals, engineering goods). Software exports are regulated by STPI. | Statutory jurisdiction trap. |
| 12 | Agmark is a mandatory quality certification for Indian electrical appliances. | Agmark certifies and grades **agricultural produce and food items** (ghee, butter, spices, honey). Electrical appliances are certified by the **Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS)** with the ISI mark. | Standards body overlap trap. |
| 13 | Pre-shipment inspection is always conducted package-by-package at the dock by customs officers. | There are **3 statutory modalities**: (1) Consignment inspection at dock, (2) In-Process Quality Control (IPQC) during manufacturing, and (3) Self-Certification by star export houses. | Quality regime nuance. |
| 14 | In Harmonized System (HS), the 8-digit code is universally identical across all 200 countries. | HS is universal globally **only up to the first 6 digits**. Digits 7 and 8 are national tariff lines determined independently by each country (e.g., Indian ITC-HS). | Universal vs National HS structure. |
| 15 | Under GRI Rule 1, you can classify goods by personal intuition before reading Chapter Notes. | Under **GRI Rule 1**, classification is governed legally by the **Terms of Headings and relative Section and Chapter Notes**. Personal intuition or commercial trade slang has zero legal standing. | Legal classification rule hierarchy. |
| 16 | GRI Rule 3(b) classifies a gift set containing cheese, wine, and crackers under the heading that occurs last numerically. | Under **Rule 3(b)**, composite goods and retail sets are classified by the component that gives them their **Essential Character** (e.g., wine in a luxury wine gift basket). Rule 3(c) (latest heading) is used only if essential character cannot be determined. | GRI Rule 3 step-wise sequence. |
| 17 | Customs Appraiser physically loads containers onto cargo vessels. | The **Appraiser examines documents, valuation, and classification**. Physical loading and seal verification at the docks is supervised by the **Preventive Officer / Superintendent**. | Customs officer role division. |
| 18 | EDPMS was created to allow private couriers to track parcel locations. | EDPMS (Export Data Processing and Monitoring System) is an **RBI statutory surveillance platform** designed to monitor cross-border foreign exchange realization and prevent trade-based money laundering. | System purpose confusion. |
| 19 | Subrogation allows an insured exporter to collect insurance money from the insurer AND also sue the carrier for double payment. | Subrogation **prevents double recovery**. Once the insurer pays the claim, the insurer acquires the exclusive right to sue the shipping carrier; the exporter cannot claim twice for the same loss. | Doctrine of Subrogation trap. |
| 20 | Forward contracts guarantee that the currency exchange rate will rise in the exporter's favour. | A forward contract **locks in a fixed exchange rate**, eliminating volatility. If the spot rate later rises above the forward rate, the exporter does NOT gain; they are bound to execute at the contracted rate. | Hedging mechanics trap. |

### 3. Top 50 Rapid-Fire Facts for Instant Recall

1. **Packing Credit tenure standard limit:** Up to 180 days (extendable to 270/360 days with approval).
2. **Benchmark interest rate for PCFC:** SOFR (Secured Overnight Financing Rate) / EURIBOR.
3. **Primary source of packing credit liquidation:** Export bills purchased, discounted, or negotiated.
4. **Can packing credit be liquidated from local currency revenue?** NO.
5. **Section of FEMA authorizing banks to deal in foreign exchange:** Section 10 of FEMA, 1999.
6. **Number of digits in bank AD Code:** 14 digits.
7. **Entity that registers AD Code at customs ports:** Indian Customs (ICES/ICEGATE).
8. **RBI system tracking export proceeds reconciliation:** EDPMS.
9. **RBI system tracking import remittance reconciliation:** IDPMS.
10. **Digital certificate issued by bank to close EDPMS entry:** e-BRC.
11. **ICC body governing international factoring standards:** Factors Chain International (FCI).
12. **Act governing factoring companies in India:** Factoring Regulation Act, 2011.
13. **Advance percentage typically granted by an Export Factor:** 70% to 85% of invoice value.
14. **Is Forfaiting done with or without recourse?** Strictly WITHOUT RECOURSE.
15. **Credit period typical for Forfaiting transactions:** 1 to 7 years (medium/long term).
16. **Instrument used in Forfaiting transactions:** Bills of Exchange or Promissory Notes.
17. **Term for a bank guarantee written on a bill of exchange:** Aval.
18. **Statute governing Indian marine insurance:** Marine Insurance Act, 1963.
19. **Standard marine cargo insurance coverage percentage:** 110% of CIF value (CIF + 10%).
20. **Widest marine insurance cargo clause:** ICC (A) - All Risks.
21. **Narrowest marine insurance cargo clause:** ICC (C) - Major casualties only.
22. **Default insurance required under Incoterms 2020 CIF:** ICC (C).
23. **Default insurance required under Incoterms 2020 CIP:** ICC (A).
24. **Are War and Strikes risks covered automatically under ICC A, B, or C?** NO; must be purchased as separate add-on clauses.
25. **Is Inherent Vice covered under ICC (A)?** NO; it is universally excluded.
26. **Rules governing General Average adjustments:** York-Antwerp Rules.
27. **Type of marine loss where cargo is voluntarily sacrificed for common safety:** General Average.
28. **Type of marine loss where accidental damage affects only one owner:** Particular Average.
29. **ECGC ownership:** 100% owned by Government of India (Ministry of Commerce).
30. **Maximum commercial risk percentage covered by ECGC SCR policy:** 90%.
31. **Maximum political risk percentage covered by ECGC SCR policy:** 90%.
32. **Does ECGC cover buyer rejection due to quality disputes?** NO.
33. **ECGC guarantee protecting commercial banks on pre-shipment loans:** Packing Credit Guarantee.
34. **Apex statute for export quality control in India:** Export (Quality Control and Inspection) Act, 1963.
35. **Headquarters of Export Inspection Council (EIC):** New Delhi.
36. **Number of statutory Export Inspection Agencies (EIAs) in India:** 5 EIAs.
37. **Quality certification agency for agricultural produce in India:** Agmark (DMI).
38. **National standards body of India:** Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS).
39. **Food safety management certification required for marine exports:** HACCP.
40. **International quality management system standard:** ISO 9001.
41. **Customs officer who grants Let Export Order (LEO):** Superintendent / Preventive Officer.
42. **Customs officer who assesses value and classification:** Appraising Officer (Appraiser).
43. **Customs document authorizing release of imported goods from port:** Out of Charge (OOC).
44. **International body that administers the Harmonized System:** World Customs Organization (WCO, Brussels).
45. **Number of digits in universal international HS code:** 6 digits.
46. **Number of digits in Indian ITC(HS) code:** 8 digits.
47. **Number of General Rules of Interpretation (GRI) in HS:** 6 Rules (Rules 1 to 6).
48. **GRI Rule establishing that heading terms and chapter notes govern:** GRI Rule 1.
49. **GRI Rule establishing that specific description beats general description:** GRI Rule 3(a).
50. **GRI Rule governing classification of composite goods by Essential Character:** GRI Rule 3(b).
