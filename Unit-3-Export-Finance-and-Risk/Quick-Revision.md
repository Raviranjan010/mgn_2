### 1. Pre-Shipment Finance & Packing Credit

- **Nature & Meaning:** Short-term working capital advance granted by an Authorized Dealer (AD) bank to an exporter to finance the procurement of raw materials, processing, manufacturing, packaging, and transporting goods up to the port of shipment.
- **Eligibility:** Available to manufacturer-exporters or merchant-exporters possessing a valid IEC and holding:
  1. A confirmed, irrevocable export order from an overseas buyer, OR
  2. A Letter of Credit (LC) opened in the exporter's favour, OR
  3. Anticipatory basis (under Red Clause LC or Running Account Facility for regular export houses).
- **Quantum & Appraisal:** Up to 80–90% of the FOB value of the export order (or domestic cost of production, whichever is lower), deducting exporter's profit margin. Bank maintains a margin requirement (usually 10–25%).
- **Disbursement:** Disbursed in stages aligned with production milestones (e.g., raw material purchase, labour charges, port transport), often paid directly to input suppliers.
- **Concessional Interest & Tenure:** Granted at concessional interest rates for up to **180 days** (extendable by AD bank up to 270/360 days in genuine cases). Supported by the government's Interest Equalisation Scheme (IES).
- **Strict Liquidation Rule:** Packing credit **CANNOT** be repaid from domestic sales or domestic cash deposits. It must be liquidated strictly out of the proceeds of the export bills purchased, discounted, or negotiated by the bank, or from proceeds credited to the exporter's EEFC (Exchange Earners' Foreign Currency) account.
- **PCFC (Packing Credit in Foreign Currency):**
  - Loan sanctioned in major foreign currencies (USD, EUR, GBP, JPY).
  - Benchmark interest rates linked to internationally recognized rates (e.g., SOFR - Secured Overnight Financing Rate / EURIBOR) instead of domestic rupee lending rates.
  - **Natural Hedge:** Protects the exporter from foreign exchange fluctuations because the loan taken in USD is repaid directly from the USD export proceeds, eliminating conversion costs.

### 2. Banking Intermediation & Regulatory Reporting

- **Authorized Dealer (AD Category-I) Banks:** Commercial banks licensed by the RBI under Section 10 of FEMA, 1999 to deal in foreign exchange. Every exporter must register an **AD Code** (14 digits) with customs.
- **Five Primary Banking Roles in EXIM:**
  1. *Issuing Bank:* Opens the LC on behalf of the buyer (applicant), pledging its own independent creditworthiness.
  2. *Advising Bank:* Located in the exporter's country; authenticates the genuineness of the LC and delivers it to the exporter without assuming financial liability.
  3. *Confirming Bank:* Adds its own independent, irrevocable payment undertaking to the LC at the issuing bank's request (absorbs issuing bank and country risk).
  4. *Negotiating / Nominated Bank:* Examines documents presented by the exporter and advances funds against compliant documents.
  5. *Collecting / Remitting Bank:* Acts under ICC URC 522 to collect payments from the buyer against commercial documents without bank credit guarantee.
- **Realization Proofs:**
  - *e-BRC (Electronic Bank Realization Certificate):* Digital certificate transmitted directly by the AD bank to the DGFT server confirming realization of foreign exchange. Essential for claiming export benefits (RoDTEP, Drawback, Advance Authorization closure).
  - *FIRC (Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate):* Physical/electronic certificate issued by banks for inward non-export remittances or service trade.
- **RBI Automated Tracking Systems:**
  - **EDPMS (Export Data Processing and Monitoring System):** Real-time web-based platform launched by RBI. Customs automatically pushes Shipping Bill data to EDPMS; AD banks upload realization details (e-BRC); the system automatically reconciles and closes open export shipping bills.
  - **IDPMS (Import Data Processing and Monitoring System):** Tracks physical import of goods against advance cross-border remittances.
- **Currency Hedging Instruments:**
  - *Forward Contract:* A binding contract with an AD bank to sell a specified amount of foreign currency on a fixed future date at an agreed exchange rate. Completely eliminates exchange risk.
  - *Currency Option:* Grants the exporter the right, but not the obligation, to sell foreign currency at a specified strike price against payment of a premium.

### 3. Export Factoring & Forfaiting Frameworks

- **Export Factoring:** A continuous financial arrangement where an exporter assigns its short-term overseas trade receivables (book debts arising on Open Account or D/A terms up to 180 days) to a specialized financial intermediary called a **Factor**.
- **Four Core Factoring Functions:**
  1. *Immediate Finance:* Advances 70–85% of invoice value immediately upon shipment.
  2. *Credit Protection:* Assumes credit risk against buyer default (in non-recourse factoring).
  3. *Sales Ledger Administration:* Maintains bookkeeping, accounting, and debtor ledgers.
  4. *Collection of Receivables:* Pursues debt collection in the buyer's country and currency.
- **The Two-Factor Mechanism (FCI Framework):**
  - **Export Factor (EF):** Located in exporter's country; provides finance and interfaces with the exporter.
  - **Import Factor (IF):** Located in buyer's country; assesses buyer creditworthiness, establishes credit limits, and handles local collection and legal recovery.
  - Governed globally by Factors Chain International (FCI).
- **Recourse vs Non-Recourse:**
  - *Recourse Factoring:* If the buyer defaults or becomes insolvent, the Factor has the right to recover the advanced money back from the exporter.
  - *Non-Recourse Factoring:* The Factor absorbs the 100% credit loss if the buyer fails to pay due to financial insolvency or protracted default.
- **Factoring vs Forfaiting:**

| Parameter | Export Factoring | Forfaiting |
|---|---|---|
| **Credit Period** | Short-term (30–180 days) | Medium to Long-term (1 to 7 years) |
| **Eligible Goods** | Consumer goods, regular commodities | Capital goods, heavy machinery, project exports |
| **Credit Instrument** | Invoices, book debts, open account | Negotiable instruments (Bills of Exchange / Promissory Notes) |
| **Bank Guarantee** | Not standard (relies on credit appraisal) | **Mandatory Aval** (bank guarantee on bill) |
| **Recourse** | Recourse or Non-Recourse | **Strictly Without Recourse (100% risk transferred)** |
| **Advance Quantum** | 70% to 85% cash advance; balance upon collection | **100% of bill value discounted** (minus discount fee) |

### 4. Marine Cargo Insurance & ECGC Coverage

- **Fundamental Legal Principles (Marine Insurance Act, 1963):**
  - *Insurable Interest:* The insured must suffer financial loss if the cargo is damaged or lost. Under CIF/FOB, insurable interest shifts along transit; exporter must have insurable interest at the time of loss to claim.
  - *Utmost Good Faith (Uberrimae Fidei):* Insured must disclose every material circumstance that influences the underwriter's risk calculation.
  - *Principle of Indemnity:* Insured is restored to their exact financial position prior to the loss; cannot profit from insurance.
  - *Doctrine of Subrogation:* Upon paying the claim, the insurer steps into the shoes of the insured to recover damages from the carrier/third party.
  - *Proximate Cause (Causa Proxima):* Insurer is liable only for losses directly caused by an insured peril, not by excluded causes (e.g., inherent vice, delay).
- **Institute Cargo Clauses (ICC) Coverage Hierarchy:**
  - **ICC (C) - Minimum Named Perils:** Fire, explosion, stranding, grounding, sinking, capsizing, collision, overturning/derailment of land conveyance, discharge of cargo at port of distress, General Average sacrifice, and jettison.
  - **ICC (B) - Intermediate Named Perils:** Covers all ICC (C) perils PLUS earthquake, volcanic eruption, lightning, washing overboard, entry of sea/lake/river water into vessel/hold/container, and total loss of any package lost overboard or dropped during loading/unloading.
  - **ICC (A) - "All Risks":** Covers all risks of loss or damage to cargo EXCEPT statutory general exclusions.
  - *Standard Exclusions (Excluded under A, B, and C):* Wilful misconduct of insured, ordinary leakage/loss in weight, improper or insufficient packing, **Inherent Vice** (natural deterioration/spoilage), insolvency of vessel owners, delay, war and strikes (unless bought back via War & Strikes Clauses).
- **General Average vs Particular Average:**
  - *General Average:* Extraordinary sacrifice or expenditure voluntarily and reasonably made in time of peril for the common safety of the maritime adventure (e.g., jettisoning cargo to refloat a stranded vessel). Loss is shared proportionately by the shipowner, cargo owners, and freight earner.
  - *Particular Average:* Accidental, partial loss caused by an insured peril affecting solely a specific cargo owner (e.g., sea water damaging 50 bales of cotton). Borne solely by the owner or insurer of that specific cargo.
- **ECGC Schemes:**
  - *SCR (Shipments Comprehensive Risks Policy):* Protects against foreign commercial risks (90% cover for buyer insolvency, default) and political risks (90% cover for war, transfer delay, import ban). Excludes trade disputes and rejection of sound goods.
  - *Financial Guarantees to Banks:* ECGC provides Packing Credit Guarantee and Post-Shipment Guarantee to banks (protecting banks up to 66.6% - 90% against exporter loan defaults).

### 5. Types of International Trade Risks & Mitigation Tools

| Risk Category | Nature of Hazard | Primary Operational Impact | Best Mitigation Tool |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Commercial Risk** | Buyer insolvency, protracted default, repudiation. | Unpaid invoices, severe working capital disruption. | Confirmed LC; Non-recourse factoring; ECGC Insurance. |
| **Political Risk** | War, civil unrest, transfer delays, exchange controls. | Goods seized abroad; payments trapped in buyer's country. | Irrevocable Confirmed LC; ECGC Political Risk Policy. |
| **Transit / Marine Risk** | Rough seas, collision, water damage, theft, pilferage. | Physical damage, total loss, package loss. | Marine Cargo Insurance (ICC A / B / C). |
| **Foreign Exchange Risk** | Adverse fluctuation in bilateral currency exchange rates. | Eroded export profit margin; currency loss. | Forward Contracts; Currency Options; PCFC financing. |
| **Legal / Compliance Risk** | Conflicting cross-border laws, regulatory embargoes, sanctions. | Contract invalidation, fines, detained goods. | CISG clauses; SIAC/ICC Arbitration; SCOMET diligence. |

### 6. Quality Inspection Regimes & Customs Operations

- **Export (Quality Control & Inspection) Act, 1963:** Apex statutory framework governing quality assurance for Indian exports.
- **Export Inspection Council (EIC) & EIAs:** EIC (apex advisory body in New Delhi) operates 5 regional **Export Inspection Agencies (EIAs)** with 40+ sub-offices across Indian maritime ports. Issues statutory **Certificate of Inspection**.
- **Three Inspection Modalities:**
  1. *Consignment-Wise Inspection:* Customs officers/EIA physically inspect samples from every individual finished export consignment before shipment.
  2. *In-Process Quality Control (IPQC):* Quality audits conducted throughout the production cycle at the manufacturing plant; finished lots cleared based on factory logs.
  3. *Self-Certification / System-Based:* Granted to established star export houses with ISO 9000 systems; exporter self-certifies compliance.
- **Voluntary & Mandatory Standards:**
  - *Agmark:* Directorate of Marketing & Inspection; grades agricultural produce (ghee, spices, honey).
  - *BIS (Bureau of Indian Standards):* National standards body; ISI mark for industrial goods.
  - *HACCP:* Hazard Analysis Critical Control Point; mandatory food safety management system for marine and processed food exports.
  - *ISO 9001:* Quality Management System; process and organizational reliability certification.
- **Customs Officers Hierarchy at Clearance:**
  - *Customs Examiner:* Physically examines cargo inside CFS/dock, verifies package marks, draws samples.
  - *Appraising Officer (Appraiser):* Reviews document valuation, ITC(HS) classification, duty exemption notifications.
  - *Superintendent / Preventive Officer:* Supervises physical gate entry, vessel loading, container seal verification; grants **Let Export Order (LEO)**.
  - *Assistant/Deputy Commissioner (AC/DC):* Adjudicates disputes, high-value assessments, bonds, and provisional clearances.

### 7. Harmonized System (HS) & GRI Rules

- **Universal Architecture:** Developed by the **World Customs Organization (WCO)**, Brussels. Universal up to **6 digits** across 200+ countries:
  - Digits 1–2: **Chapter** (e.g., Chapter 09 = Coffee, Tea, Mate and Spices).
  - Digits 3–4: **Heading** (e.g., 09.02 = Tea, whether or not flavoured).
  - Digits 5–6: **Subheading** (e.g., 0902.10 = Green tea in immediate packings <= 3 kg).
  - Digits 7–8: **Indian National Tariff Item (ITC-HS)** (e.g., 0902.10.10 = Content not exceeding 25g).
- **General Rules of Interpretation (GRI Rules 1 to 6):**
  - **Rule 1:** Classification is determined strictly according to the **Terms of Headings** and relative Section/Chapter Notes (governs > 90% of trade).
  - **Rule 2(a):** Incomplete/unfinished goods are classified as the finished article if they possess the *essential character* (e.g., unassembled bicycle kit).
  - **Rule 2(b):** Mixtures and combinations of materials.
  - **Rule 3 (When goods are classifiable under two or more headings):**
    - *3(a) Specific over General:* The heading providing the most specific description takes precedence.
    - *3(b) Essential Character:* Mixtures/composite goods classified by the material providing the essential character.
    - *3(c) Latest Heading:* If 3(a) and 3(b) fail, classify under the heading that occurs last in numerical order.
  - **Rule 4:** Goods that cannot be classified under Rules 1–3 are classified under the heading appropriate to goods to which they are *most akin*.
  - **Rule 5:** Packaging: 5(a) Specially fitted cases (guitar cases, camera cases) classified with article; 5(b) Repetitive durable packaging containers.
  - **Rule 6:** Comparison of subheadings at the same digit level.
